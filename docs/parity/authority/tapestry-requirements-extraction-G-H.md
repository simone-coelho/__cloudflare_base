## G. How each expectation meets the engine

Organised on the scope split above. G-A is what we need them to feed the engine, the answer to "what do you expect us to feed you": at arrival, per piece of content, per module, per product, the events, identity. G-B is track 4: the engine's logic and the levers, against each of Mandeep's questions. G-C is the engine's data contract with their front end. ### G-A. What we need them to feed the engine

Source: the metadata and learning investigation of 7 October, read on `rem/integration` at `b0aef94` (W16 to W40 merged locally; W41 built on `rem/w41-build` at `7f5ceb9` and in review; no P ticket started), with the arrival-signal findings from the front-end investigation. This is the answer to Mandeep's question in all its forms: at arrival, per piece of content, per module, per product, the events, identity, and the one engine-dependent item on his "activate now" list.

**At arrival: what the page sends, what we derive, and the one convention we need.**

- The page sends two things: its page name, and the shopper's consent choice, which their consent tool posts to the SDK (a grant never comes from the snapshot; with no choice recorded the shopper is on the default arm). The SDK adds the page-view nonce, the UTM and referrer fields and the browsing session on its own.
- The engine derives the rest at the edge: region from the request's geolocation; the entry channel, classified once per visit from UTM medium, source and term and the referrer host into six words; the visit bucket (first, second to third, fourth and later) from its own count with a thirty-minute idle rule; the journey stage from behaviour; affinity, the holdout arm and identity (`visit.ts:30–37, 121–235`, `cell.ts:15–72`, `decisions.ts:850`). Channel needs tracking consent; visit, stage and affinity need personalization consent.
- **The recommendation we owe them:** a campaign convention. Whether the engine can tell TikTok from Instagram from brand search depends on what their UTM parameters carry, or on their passing `channel` from their own classification as the fallback the engine accepts. Today the channel vocabulary is six words fixed in code, with the campaign term and the referrer network available to seed rules beside it. If their taxonomy is richer, the choice is to map it onto the six words with the campaign term carrying the detail, or to make the channel vocabulary a tenant setting, which is three to four days under the W25 cell grammar. Section H carries it.

**Per piece of content: the ten things, grounded in the validator** (`kinds.ts:82–170`, `types.ts:13–33`).

1. **Content ID:** theirs, stable through edits and republishing, the same in the export, on the page and in every event.
2. **Title.**
3. **Format:** on-model, silo, detail, flat-lay, video, user-generated or editorial, from the agreed list.
4. to 7. **Tags on the agreed dimensions, from the agreed value list:** line or collection, category or silhouette, occasion, price band. Sparse is fine: AI proposes from their list and their team approves every tag.
8. **Where it may appear:** the module or slot identifiers it may fill.
9. **When it is live:** status, publish date, expiry date.
10. **Market and language,** as a tag.

Optional, each buying one behaviour: the journey stages it suits, featured product identifiers (links and credit only; the piece inherits no product attribute), a freshness date, an in-stock flag, a render reference. To be served at all a piece needs items 1, 2, 3 and 8; to be personalized it needs 4 to 7 as well. Channel, visit number and location are not content attributes; the engine reads them from the visit. Any other field is dropped and named in the diagnostics. One catalogue holds modules and content together, about two thousand pieces in two megabytes.

Three things our documents promise as fields that the schema handles differently, to say plainly rather than discover later: **brand** is the tenant and its catalogue, not a field; **locale** is a tag plus a slot exclusion; **market or rights restrictions** beyond the date window exist as a per-piece eligibility rule on the demo branch only (`eligibleWhen`, commit `82f41b8d`), not on `rem/integration`. The render reference is stored and never returned; the front end renders by its own identifier, or we return it, half a day. Explaining these is no build; adding a locale field and region eligibility is about a day each.

**Where the vocabulary lives, and how a tag is approved.** The dimension keys come from the tenant's published registry, at most 32, each naming the event field it reads; a customer registry is written by hand (`configStore.ts:299–337`). The values are not held in the registry: the published catalogue's own tag values are the vocabulary on every dimension it tags, matched exactly and case-sensitively, and they also decide which event values may build affinity (`service.ts:356–400`, `core.ts:451–471`). The only closed value list with meanings is the enrichment taxonomy. AI-proposed tags follow W19: generate within the taxonomy (a label outside it fails the run), capture immutably with provenance, one approve, edit or reject per label by an operator, export only what was approved or edited, publish as a new catalogue revision with a receipt (`enrichmentGeneration.ts:79–88`, `enrichment.ts:117–254`, `enrichmentPublication.ts:19–67`). Their own tagger's output enters the same flow, marked unverified. Design question for H: whether a published value list (their T7 answer) should become the authority instead of the catalogue's tags, about two days.

**Per module: the ten things.** There is no module schema today; the mechanism already runs on catalogue pieces (a layout is a slot whose items are module pieces, `take` 1 to 50, returned in order with de-duplication, exclusions, off-limits and a pinned prefix; the Vantage demo does exactly this, showing six of seven). P02's three fields map onto existing piece fields: the identifier, the kind as `type` plus a tag, the journey stages as `journeyStageFit`. What a module should carry so that affinity can match it is tags on registered dimensions weighted by the layout slot, and in particular **a module-type dimension of its own** (reviews, user-generated, material story, craftsmanship story, recommendations, complete the look, category grid, recently viewed, editorial), kept separate from content format so that "which story" and "which image" stay the two problems Mandeep named. A module without an explicit content-type tag scores on its `type` string as if that were a content type, so the tag must be set deliberately (`typeAffinity.ts:12–28`).

1. **Module ID,** stable; keep the page out of it if the module appears on several pages.
2. **Name,** in their words.
3. **Module type,** from the agreed list.
4. **The pages it appears on,** and its position today.
5. **Its rule:** free to move, held at a position, never moved, or may be hidden.
6. **What it is about,** where that is fixed, as tags on the agreed dimensions.
7. **The journey stages it suits.**
8. **What counts as using it:** a tile click, a swipe, opening the reviews.
9. **When it has nothing to show,** and how the page tells us.
10. **Later:** one sentence of purpose for the semantic vector, and which content may fill it.

Items 8 and 9 are the two the engine cannot infer. Module events are P03. "Nothing to show" is not designed: the engine has no per-request eligibility, so either the page hides an empty module and reports it (no engine work), or `eligibleWhen` with a page-sent signal is merged from the demo branch (about a day), or P02's module gets a `requires` field (about a day).

**Per product.** A customer tenant holds no product catalogue in the engine; an event teaches only what it carries, and an unknown product with no attributes is dropped (`demos/registry.ts:220–225`, `core.ts:491–506`). So the product feed is used to agree the value list and to confirm that the identifiers in events are the identifiers in SFCC and the feed; it is not loaded. Product view, add to bag and purchase must carry the attribute values on the agreed dimensions. One caution to state early: once content is published, its tag values become the authority for each dimension it covers, and a product value the content does not carry stops building interest on that dimension. Either content is tagged across the full value list, or the published list becomes the authority (the H item above).

**The events, and what each teaches.**

- Product view, save, add to bag, purchase: raise interest on the product's values, by the behaviour weights. Built.
- Content impression, click, dwell, video: on a slot measured as `rendered-v1`; a click teaches the piece's content type and feeds the regional shares (`contentTelemetry.ts:62–126`). Built. Today using a piece or a module teaches the profile only its format, not its other tags; inheriting tags per dimension is one to two days (H).
- Module impression and engagement, "as they define it": P03.
- Order completed, with order identifier, items, value, currency: for the holdout comparison and for credit. Under the default `direct` attribution an add to bag credits a module only when the event names the module, its decision identifier or a product the module featured; under `any`, one decision per slot takes the credit (`policy.ts:193–216`). **The recommendation:** their data layer puts the module identifier on commerce events that originate inside a module, or each module reports the products it showed; otherwise the layout learns from engagement and reports commerce (H).

**Identity.** Their hashed identifier as the account identifier and the hook to pass it before recognition are P12 and P13; today `identify()` always links and needs the backend-signed statement. The two consent choices must be posted by the page.

**Affinity-based audiences, the engine-dependent item on his "activate now" list.** Built: interest per dimension and value from product behaviour alone, with no content catalogue published (every value on a registered dimension builds interest); membership of `dimension_value_affinity` audiences entering at 0.6 and leaving below 0.45; operator-published named audiences with conditions on interest values; the segments and interest values pushed to the page on every update; the ODP connector that forwards events through the tenant's action mapping, writes interest values to profile fields on every membership change and reads ODP segment qualification back (`core.ts:181–264, 466–471`, `operator.ts:113–151`, `odpLoop.ts:174–554`). W41 adds restoring those interests from ODP at sign-in, in review. Demo-only: audience names minted from a catalogue, and creating Optimizely Experimentation audiences and flags through the REST API, which runs only on demo deployments with shared credentials and answers 404 on a customer deployment (`optimizelyFx.ts:61–104`, `index.ts:192–226`). Not designed: the identity that lets Experimentation see our ODP segments. We write profiles under a digest of tenant, namespace and our shopper identifier; Experimentation reads ODP segments for its own user identifier; unless the two line up, an experiment cannot target our segments. Options: send their hashed identifier as a second ODP identifier beside P12, one to two days; have the page pass our segments and interest values to their Experimentation user context as attributes, no engine work and about a day of their front end; expose our ODP identifier to their SDK, a day plus a privacy review. Nothing creates ODP fields or segments: "mirroring" means their ODP owner builds segments on our fields (T28).

What must exist first: the Tapestry tenant on its stamp (W37); the registry published (T6 to T8); the SDK on their pages with consent wired (T19, T33); product events carrying the agreed values with matching identifiers (T20, T23, T24); for ODP, a tenant connector entry and a secret binding, which is a deployment change needing owner authority, their ODP owner's fields and segments (T27, T28) and privacy approval (T29); for experiment targeting, aligned identifiers or attributes passed on the page. Not needed: content metadata, or the front end drawing a returned order.

**[ANNOTATION]** This refines what Simone said on the call. "Not until the content is tagged" is right for content personalization and for content-type interest. Audiences built on product interest are gated by the attributes carried on product events, the SDK and consent, not by content metadata. Near-term means once the SDK is on their pages, in the integration phase, not at signature. It is the honest shape of track 1's one engine-dependent item, and it answers "we didn't pay for something we won't use for six months" without promising tomorrow.

### G-B. Track 4: the engine's logic and the levers

Source: the layout and levers investigation of 7 October, read on `rem/integration` at `b0aef948` (W16 to W40 delivered locally, W41 active, no P ticket started). Day figures are rough build-and-test effort for one lane, not dates. Evidence paths at the end of the section.

**C1, "which modules to show at all, and in what order."**

- Built: a layout slot that returns a fixed-size subset. `take` is how many items a slot returns; the composer ranks every eligible item that names the slot and serves the top `take`. Register M modules with `take` K below M and the rest are not returned; which ones drop varies by shopper and context through the score (`kinds.ts:263`, `contentCompose.ts:316–339`). Modules not on today's page are candidates the moment they are registered against the slot; with no signal every score is zero and ties fall to catalogue order, so the first K modules in the document are today's page. A hidden module earns evidence only through exploration, which draws from all eligible candidates.
- Not designed: a variable count per shopper (everyone gets `take`; fewer come back only when fewer are eligible, and eligibility reads neither the shopper nor the context), and exclusion by context or shopper state. There is no score threshold; a zero score is served as `default`. A per-piece `eligibleWhen` rule exists on the demo branch only (`feature/real-time-personalization`, commit `82f41b8d`, from the QVC demo) and is not on `rem/integration`.
- The engine cannot know that a module is empty. The parity document's own example, "Recently viewed: not shown, nothing to show", needs the page to say so; otherwise the engine returns the module and, under the default `served-v1` basis, counts an exposure that drew nothing.
- P02 as written ("returns all N module IDs in ranked order") never exercises selection. The smallest reconciliation: P02 becomes "K positions over M ≥ K registered modules; returns up to K in rank order; a module not returned is not drawn"; layout slots measured on `rendered-v1`; a P01 per-context order may list fewer than K modules, and an unlisted module is hidden in that context.
- Options for visibility that varies by arrival: **A**, fixed K of M by rank, half a day on P02's wording, tests and the kit; asks that every candidate module be registered and drawable, a K per page, "draw exactly what is returned", render acknowledgments, and an exploration share so hidden modules earn evidence. **B**, a visible set per context inside P01, half a day to a day on P01; asks for each channel × visit bucket which modules show and in what order. **C**, a per-module eligibility rule by context and state, porting `eligibleWhen` and adding the engine's own channel, visit bucket and stage plus a page-sent "module is empty" flag, one and a half to two days; asks one rule per module and that the page report empty modules.

**C3, "levers at page level or module level, and where the goal is set."** Both answers on the call were partly right. The decision and its goal sit on the layout slot, one per page, which is the page's layout setting; the scoring inputs and the controls sit on each module; the page itself carries nothing and only groups slots (`types.ts:154–159`).

Mandeep's levers, mapped:

- **Channel "with a weight".** Channel is not a scoring dimension. It enters through seed rules on a slot, mapping `entry_channel` to module tags at a weight from 0 to 1 (W16, built), and as level 1 of the learned ladder (built). Nothing sets how much channel counts against the shopper's behaviour; a population prior per channel × visit is P07 (planned), and a blend constant on it, like the regional K, is half a day more.
- **Visit number.** Acts only as level 2 of the learned ladder and in prior cells; it is not a seed signal. A configured order per channel × visit bucket is P01 (planned); visit as a seed signal is half a day.
- **The objective.** Set per slot: `reward` and `objective`, default click and unit. The layout slot carries its own, separate from the content slots inside its modules. Crediting, however, follows one brand-wide attribution policy: under the default `direct` match an add-to-bag credits a module only when the event names the module, its decision id or one of its featured products; under `any`, one decision per slot per outcome takes the whole page conversion. Nothing designed splits a page conversion across the modules in view, which the 18 September extraction flagged as G11(ii). P03 adds module events and P16 a success event per piece; neither splits a page outcome. Options: **A**, module-tied credit only, where conversions made inside a module carry its decision id and buy-box conversions do not train the layout, half a day after P03; **B**, shared credit across the modules served on the page view, equal or discounted by position, about two days, with their data science agreeing the split; **C**, the module engaged last before the conversion, one to one and a half days after P03, needing the module engagement event (Phase 1 T8).
- **The trust dial.** γ per slot, 0 to 1, default 0, built. The percentage form is P09 (planned).
- **Pins and off-limits per module.** Pins are an ordered prefix from position 1 (W20, built); off-limits applies to a whole slot; exclusion by id is per slot. A pin at any position and a single fixed module are P04 (planned).
- **Exploration.** Per slot, off by default, hashed on visitor, slot and hour; it moves one candidate to first place. Adjacent swaps with a position discount are P05 (planned).

**C2, "if they don't mention channel at all."** Built, through W16 and W25. The cell dimensions today are channel (six words), visit bucket (1, 2 to 3, 4 and later, unknown), journey stage, region and leading affinity, pooled coarsest first: everyone, then channel, then channel and visit, then stage, then region, then leading affinity (`cell.ts:44–72`, `stats.ts:36–50`). For each module separately, the finest level whose decayed exposures plus prior strength reach nMin (30) answers, shrunk toward the slot's own rate in that cell, itself shrunk toward its parent; modules in one layout can answer from different levels. Region is also a population prior on the base, with its own region, country, everyone ladder, off for a tenant whose learn document omits it. So if nobody configures channel: with no seed rule, channel does nothing for a first visitor; learning still splits by channel by itself wherever a channel cell reaches nMin per module, and pools to everyone otherwise; that learned split acts only with γ above zero and a non-zero base; channel is `unknown` without tracking consent; the ladder order and the channel and visit vocabularies are fixed in code. The recommendation for the week of 26 October follows: optimise by channel and visit first, with a configured starting order per context (P01) and the channel × visit prior (P07), because the ladder already learns on them.

**"Is the layout per shopper or per context?"** The terms that depend on the individual shopper: her affinity against the slot's weights; the regional prior's blend, whose weight is hers; the journey-stage rule; fatigue from her decision ring; which ladder level answers (levels 3 to 5 include her stage and leading interest); the exploration bucket and the holdout arm, both hashed on her; and consent. The terms that depend only on context: the seeds; the learned lift at levels 0 to 2; imported priors per cell; freshness and merchandising; pins, exclusions, `take` and catalogue order; and, once built, the configured order (P01) and the channel × visit prior (P07). No context-only mode is declared. The closest configuration today is γ at zero, the layout slot weighted on a dimension no shopper interest is built on, seeds by channel, campaign and referrer, and stage, fatigue and exploration off, which makes the order a function of entry channel, campaign term, referrer network and arm; visit cannot enter it. P10 covers only shoppers below the floor, and N is set per tenant. Options: **A**, a per-slot context-only switch that ignores affinity, stage and fatigue, stops the ladder at channel × visit (optionally region), hashes exploration on context and hour, and records exposure of the cached layout through a light per-view beacon, two to three days; **B**, P10 set per slot with "never" allowed on the layout slot, half a day on P10, with the P01 orders written; **C**, cache the module fragments and call for the order on each view, no engine work. This is the same question as G-C's first design question, seen from the engine's side.

**C4, "there has to be a reason the layout did not change."** The per-module explanation is built: each served position records authority (engine, pin or default), the drivers, base and final score, the regional and seed contributions, the full learned term with the level that answered, exploration, stage, freshness, fatigue, diversity, the top ten candidates with scores, the cell, arm and versions (`types.ts:397–494`, `decide.ts:533–573`). The receipt already says: pinned; holdout; consent off; "no interest signal yet, catalogue order"; "not applied (trust 0)"; "moved this score by nothing"; "nothing learned yet"; explored; short take (`receipts.ts:105–178`). What is missing, and not designed: a layout-level answer that compares the served order with the baseline and gives one reason for the whole layout; γ recorded only when some level qualified, so "nothing learned yet" does not say the n it has or the nMin it needs; no reason for "below the signal floor" (P10 unbuilt) or "nothing else was eligible"; an off-limits slot writes no record at all; and the sentence "no interest signal yet for this shopper" is printed when she does have interests but none on the dimensions the layout weights, which is exactly the Simone-and-James case. P06 covers only the trust-dial cause, and presumes the served order at γ zero is the baseline, which today it is not: affinity, seeds, the regional prior, stage, freshness and fatigue all reorder at γ zero, so "the page keeps your order" (parity §3.5) holds only when the layout slot weights nothing a shopper carries. Options: **A**, a layout reason code, comparing the served order with the baseline (catalogue order, later the P01 order) and emitting "unchanged" with closed reasons (pinned; off-limits; holdout or consent; below the signal floor; trust at zero; no level reached nMin, with the n; zero base; ties broken by catalogue order; nothing else eligible) on the decision set, each record and the receipt, and correcting the wrong sentence, about two days; **B**, a two-visitor comparison view in the console, one to one and a half days after A; **C**, P06 widened to a full shadow, serve the baseline and record the order with every term, one day on P06, with launch described as "shadow" rather than "trust dial at zero".

**[ANNOTATION]** The last point is a story correction we owe ourselves before the week of 26 October. "Your page is the baseline and the trust dial starts at zero" has been our sentence in every document, including the four-questions document Mandeep holds. The accurate form: at launch, their order is held by configuration (pins, zero weights or the P01 orders) while the engine records; the trust dial governs only how far the learned term may move it. Which of the three options becomes the launch mode is a design decision for Friday or the week after, not a problem.

**The dismiss preference, for phase two by Mandeep's own word.** Not designed. Behaviour weights are validated to 0 to 1000 and affinity is floored at zero, so a dismiss cannot lower interest; learning counts successes only (`configStore.ts:343–355`, `core.ts:221–244`). Options: **A**, per-shopper suppression, a dismiss event storing the module on her record with an expiry, with consent, retention and erasure applying, two to three days; **B**, a negative behaviour weight for named actions, subtracted with a floor at zero, one and a half to two days; **C**, a population dismiss counter that lowers the module's lift in its context, one and a half to two days.

**C3, "the admin capability a strategist needs."** Built: a console with sign-in, roles and a fifteen-minute token, where writes need an operator grant for the tenant. It edits the interests (behaviour weights, decay, thresholds, the registry), the slot rules (`take`, pins, off-limits, excluded ids and tags, allowed types, dimension weights on registry dimensions, stage, freshness, fatigue, diversity, merchandising), the dials per slot (γ, reward, objective, measurement basis, exploration) and per brand (holdout, attribution policy, n0, decay, lift clamps, nMin, the regional prior), the learning grid (freeze or reject per module), with history and rollback; every save is a versioned revision in force within the thirty-second publication cache, with no deploy (`public/console/views-config.js:141–185, 443–579`, `src/config/publication.ts:8`). Published documents only, with no editor yet: seed rules, imported priors, the content catalog that registers modules, weights on dimensions outside the registry. Planned, not there yet: P01 per-context orders, P05 rotation, P09 the percentage dial, P10 the signal floor, P11 floors per page type, P14 weights per product type. Fixed in code, so a change needs a deploy: the ladder order and depth, the visit buckets and the thirty-minute gap, the six channel words and their classification, the seed signals, the reward list, the exploration modes, the ten recorded candidates, the zero floor on behaviour weights. Options for the gaps: a seed-rule editor in slot rules, one day; a per-context order editor built with P01, one day on P01; tenant settings for the ladder order and depth, the visit buckets and the channel vocabulary, three to four days including a cell-grammar version bump and a prior re-export under W25.

**C7, his vector reading, and the Friday explanation.** The profile is a sparse vector with named coordinates: for every value of every registered dimension a decayed score read as a = R / (R + K), at most 32 dimensions with 24 values each in the shipped tuning (`core.ts:117–132, 289–298`). Context is not a coordinate: channel, visit, stage and region choose which evidence and rules apply, and region also enters as a population prior blended into a new shopper's interests. The match is a weighted sum: for each tag the item carries, the shopper's coordinate times the slot's weight for that dimension, summed; rule terms added, merchandising limits applied, the result multiplied by lift to the power γ; every term on the receipt (`contentCompose.ts:253–264`, `types.ts:445–491`). Dense similarity is planned, not built: cosine between an item's stored vector (P18) and the shopper's decayed centroid (P20) as one term at weight zero (P19), nearest-piece priors (P21), human-approved concept vectors (P22); and P19 must not reopen the external-model path W34 withdrew. No user-embedding model and no shopper-to-shopper matching, by design (parity §4.6).

In two paragraphs, for Simone to give:

> Each shopper is a vector, and every module is scored by how well it matches it, but the vector is a list of named interests, not an embedding. For every value of every dimension you register (occasion: evening, content type: video, category: shoulder bags) the shopper has a number between 0 and 1. It rises with what they do, weighted by the behaviour weights you set, and fades with time. Each module and piece of content carries tags on the same dimensions. Its score is the sum of the shopper's numbers on its tags, each multiplied by the weight that slot gives the dimension. That sum is the similarity match, and the receipt prints every term of it. Where the shopper comes from, channel, visit number, region, is not part of the vector. It is the context of the decision. It decides which of your rules fire and which learned evidence applies. For someone we have never seen, it also supplies the starting point: what shoppers in that region engage with today and, as we add it, what shoppers arriving the same way engage with.
>
> The learning sits beside the profile. For every module in every context, the engine counts how often the module was shown and how often it was used, and turns that into a lift against the page's average. It works from paid social on a first visit, to paid social overall, to everyone, so a thin context borrows from a broader one until it has its own evidence. The lift moves the module's score through a trust dial you control. The dial starts at zero, so on day one the evidence is recorded and shown but moves nothing. We deliberately do not train a model of each shopper or match shoppers to other shoppers. With eight to ten modules and a few visits per person, a per-person model would be guessing. Learning by context answers the same question, what do people like this one do, in a form you can read, tune and reset. Meaning-based vectors arrive later as one more weighted line on the same receipt, starting at zero.

**"How do you know this module works for this user?"** The honest answer: the design learns two things, each person's interests and each module's performance per context; it keeps no per-person score for each module, and at this traffic per person that number would be noise. "We learn 'works for people arriving like you' and 'matches what you are interested in'." Two things in the code sharpen it: using a module today teaches the profile only its format, not its other tags (`contentTelemetry.ts:111–126`); and there is no per-person negative signal. Options, if a per-person module preference is wanted in phase one: **A**, a module-type dimension plus engagements that carry the module's tags into the profile, an explainable per-person preference by module type, two to three days after P02 and P03, needing their module-type list and tags; **B**, a negative per-person signal (shown and ignored, or dismissal later), about two days plus a privacy check; **C**, their own model as one weighted term, which reopens the path W34 withdrew and is new scope with its own approvals.

**Design questions for Friday, from the engine's side:**

1. Which modules show at all: a fixed K of M by rank (today); a visible set per channel × visit inside P01; or a per-module rule by context and state, with the page reporting "nothing to show". And is "draw exactly what we return" the front-end contract?
2. What "launch" means for the layout: a full shadow (serve their order, record ours with every term), or γ at zero, under which affinity, seeds and the regional prior already move modules. And how a configured order (P01) or the channel × visit prior (P07) gives the learned lift a base to multiply: a rank-derived base score, an additive learned term on layout slots, or orders as pins that learning never moves. This is N20 in document 35 and a model choice for both data-science teams.
3. Where a page conversion lands: module-tied events only (P03); credit shared across the modules in view; or the module engaged last before the conversion. And whether the attribution policy stays brand-wide.
4. Whether channel and visit need their own weight: a blend constant on the P07 prior; visit as a seed signal; or configured orders only.

### G-C. The engine's data contract with their front end

Source: the front-end investigation of 7 October, read on `rem/integration` (SDK 0.2.0, the routes, the composer), with the SDK package run in Node against a synthetic host for the timing statements. Full evidence paths are listed at the end of this section.

**C6, "what will you give me so that I can paint the right module?"**

- One call, `POST /v1/{tenant}/decisions/snapshot`, returns every slot of the page as ordered lists of their own identifiers, with no markup. A layout slot and a content slot have the same shape: a layout is a slot whose items are modules, its list is the sequence, and a `take` below the number of eligible modules is the selection. So Mandeep's two halves of the experience problem, which modules and in what order, are one list in the payload. Nothing registers a slot as "layout" in product code yet; P02 adds that (`contentCompose.ts:324–334`, `kinds.ts:263`).
- Per decision: our `contentId`, their `customerContentId`, `type`, `slot`, a page-wide `order` counter, `score`, `strategy` (`affinity`, `default`, `tenant-pinned`), `explain.drivers`, and the receipt (`decisionId`, `renderOffer`) when tracking consent, a `rendered-v1` slot and a page instance are all present (`service.ts:648–657`).
- The engine sends no default asset. An off-limits slot, a refused pin prefix or a slot with nothing eligible is absent from the answer, and the SDK hands that slot's subscriber an empty list; the page paints its own default (`listen.ts:130–138`). With no signal the engine's own default is catalogue order, marked `strategy: "default"`.
- The payload does not tell the page its context key: `cell`, `visitor_id`, `session_id`, `regional` are not returned, although the older kit's example shows them. The newer kit on `rem/integration` is the copy to restate from.

**"How many slots and what options": the input.** The page sends the page name and the consent choice. Everything else is derived: the SDK adds the page-view nonce, the UTM and referrer fields and the browsing session; the engine derives region from the edge request's geolocation, the entry channel from the visit's own entry (UTM medium, source, term, referrer host), the visit number from its own count with a thirty-minute idle rule, the journey stage, affinity, the holdout arm and identity (`decisions.ts:828–850`, `visit.ts:151–235`, `service.ts:456–513`). The page may pass `channel` as a fallback only. Slot lists are never sent at runtime: the published slots document decides every slot on a page and its options (`take`, `allowedTypes`, weights, pins, exclusions, `offLimits`; `content/types.ts:67–103`). Their hashed identifier is not an input today: P12 and P13.

**[ANNOTATION]** This is the clean answer to "what do you expect us to feed you at arrival": the page name, the consent choice, and a campaign convention. Entry channel is read from UTM and referrer, so whether the engine can tell TikTok from Instagram from brand search depends on their UTM discipline, or on their passing `channel` from their own classification. G-A carries that as a recommendation.

**C6, "my caching is going to break."** What holds for every option: no decision response is cacheable (`Cache-Control: no-store`, receipts bound to the shopper and the page view for five minutes, the visitor identifier inside the decision identifier). Cached HTML can therefore hold the order only; receipts always come from a per-shopper browser call after paint. Today the only constant variants are the default arm (no consent, holdout, unreadable record) and a layout with pins only or zero weights. Everything else varies per shopper, including affinity at trust dial zero, because gamma gates only the learned lift (`decide.ts:384`).

**C6, "above the fold constant."** Built today, through W20: `pinnedPieceIds` are the required first positions, reserved page-wide before ranking, identical on every arm, never explored; ranking fills the rest (`content/types.ts:72–73`, `contentCompose.ts:159–249`). Two caveats to state when it is offered: one ineligible pinned module refuses the whole slot, so the page's default must be the same top; and pins are a prefix only, with mid-page pins and per-module off-limits in P04.

**Returning today's order while learning (positions 19 and 20).** Trust dial zero does not hold today's order by itself, because affinity still ranks. Today it means pinning all N modules or zero weights with the modules in catalogue order; P06 adds the shadow order at gamma zero. This is configuration, not a build, and it is what "merge before the freeze returning today's page" requires.

**Mid-session.** Nothing pushes a decision set in production, as position 10 says: the SDK re-asks after every acknowledged interaction and on socket reconnect, and re-delivers a changed slot (`listen.ts:341–343`). Measured consequences for a layout: a page that holds the old layout loses the receipts of moved modules, so their clicks are not credited; and because `order` is page-wide, a layout that shows one module fewer shifts every later content slot and those slots re-fire. A hold policy is needed for layouts, and receipt keys per slot position would stop the ripple. Both are design items, days each.

**Round trips.** Every page load makes two sequential calls before the first decision, session then snapshot; a third when the stored session has expired. The kit's own ten-line example then calls `refresh()` and `connect()` back to back, so the first answer is discarded and re-asked and the deadline restarts: with a 300 ms snapshot the first decision landed at about 600 ms in the measurement. Two fixes, both ours: P13 serves the first decision on the first request, and the SDK should apply the in-flight answer then refresh rather than discard it. Until then the integration note is: `connect()` and `pageView()` after the first delivery.

**Nitin's two paths, from the code.** Enabling what exists today: CSP and WebSocket allow-listing, the SDK with Web Locks in the browser matrix, the consent choice posted by their consent tool (until then every shopper is on the default arm), an explicit `pageView`, the commerce events or the GA4 data-layer mapping, `identify()` with the backend HMAC, `rendered()` on content slots. The experience change: a template that draws the module region from the returned list, hides modules not returned and closes the gap; containers marked per module; a render and caching pattern per region; hold and late-answer rules; content inside modules as sibling slots today with page-wide de-duplication, nesting in P26.

**Documents to correct before anything goes to their developers.** The kit on this branch is older than the one on `rem/integration` and shows fields the route does not return; the implementation guide's "single request" (§3.1), `listen.apply` (§5.2, §5.8) and "the page will never exceed the deadline" (§5.8) are not what the code does; the long four-tracks document's "the SDK reads your consent framework" (§4.6) should say the page posts the choice, and its "never repainted without asking" (§4.2) should say the SDK re-asks and delivers to the page's subscriber, which decides; neither sentence is in the four-questions document Mandeep holds. The CFO document's "delivered over a persistent connection" describes the designed frame shape (position 10: a pushed decision set is supported, with no production sender yet); today each decision set is re-fetched over HTTPS after the live channel signals an update.

**Design questions for the front-end track**, phrased for Simone and James, not for the kickoff:

1. Per context or per shopper first? Context-only for the first N actions (P01 and P10) with HTML cached per page × channel × visit bucket; per shopper from day one; or a constant above-the-fold with a per-shopper tail.
2. Who computes the cache key before HTML? An engine-issued, non-identifying context cookie or header; their CDN classifying the channel and keeping its own visit counter; or a shopper-free, cacheable context-layout read.
3. How do holdout, consent, exploration and region enter the key? A separate default variant; uncached for holdout and non-consented shoppers; rotation (P05) below the fold only.
4. The "three versions": one pinned prefix; above-the-fold and below-the-fold slots; templates as items of a `take: 1` slot; or P01 per-context orders. And which modules may be hidden at all.
5. Hold within a page view: an SDK hold that keeps receipts; an engine answer sticky per page view; or per-slot positions in receipt keys.
6. The first-paint path: the snapshot mints the session (P13); the session answer carries the first decision; or the SDK applies the in-flight answer then refreshes.
7. Server-side on Coach? If yes: package the bridge; forward entry and region on server calls; set the timeout and bootstrap lifetime; define adoption when a module has nothing to show.
8. How today's order is returned while learning: pin all N; zero weights in catalogue order; or the shadow order (P06). And how cached orders are invalidated on publish.
