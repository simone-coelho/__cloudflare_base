| | Which scope | What it answers | Does it exist? |

You are not the lead on that, and you are right to say so. What you *do* own is the technical scope input that the SOW is built from: deliverable definitions, acceptance criteria, customer dependencies, and the timeline logic. That division is standard, and stating it once should end the thread.

## What already exists versus what is genuinely missing

**Exists and is reusable today:**

- Deliverables — Implementation Plan, W1 through W12
- Milestones — M0 through M6
- Customer obligations — D1 through D9
- Acceptance bar for v1 — one container, one page, 20–30 assets, verifiably different content per visitor, weights live-tunable, every decision explainable
- Out-of-scope list — in the capability breakdown
- Effort units for services — the PS Delivery Guide

**Genuinely missing, and this is the short list:**

1. **Per-deliverable acceptance criteria** in contract form. Milestones tell you when; an SOW needs what counts as done, item by item.
2. **Customer obligations written as obligations**, not as a dependency table. D1–D9 need converting into "Tapestry will provide X by Y" language, because that is what protects the dates.
3. **A change-control clause.** Nothing currently says what happens when scope moves — and it has already moved twice this month.
4. **Reconciliation of two open items** before anything can be locked: the pilot brand, and the Oct 1 / Nov 1 dates against the November freeze.

## The artifact that ends this

A **two-page Scope Anchor**, assembled from the above:

1. What we deliver — the numbered deliverable list
2. What "done" means for each — acceptance criteria
3. What Tapestry provides, by when
4. What is explicitly not included
5. Milestone dates
6. How scope changes get handled

Nothing new gets invented. It is assembly plus the four missing pieces. That is the document the customer anchors on and procurement lifts from, and it can be done quickly once the pilot brand and the freeze date are settled.
