## How to read the tables, and the rules that bind reuse

**Columns.** *What it says* is an exact quotation. *Who* is the speaker or the kind of document. *Where* is file and line. *Simplest wording* is the plainest phrase a source already uses for the idea, quoted, or a dash where none exists. *Today* is for the lead only and never appears as a label in a marketer's or seller's piece (the owner's ruling, `docs/enablement/LANE-BRIEF-COMMON.md:106-112`). Its words are S2's and S5's: **Built**, **Designed** (in the current design: the parity document, the vector design, Phase 1, tickets P01 to P27), **Demo only** (usually Generation 1: the Coach storefront, Meridian/Calder or The Bright Hour on the shared worker at `26e2db1`; the product is Generation 2, `docs/demos/BOOTSTRAP.md:95-115`), **Open**, **Withdrawn**, and two of my own: **Not held** (named somewhere, but in no design or code) and **Words** (a customer's, a partner's or our own positioning, not a capability). S5 row numbers (A4, V7, C1 and so on) point to the product capability map, `docs/enablement/research/S5-product-as-designed.md` §1.

**Six rules that bind any reuse of this material.**

1. **Facts.** S2 is the engineering view, pinned to today's code; S5 is the product as designed, which the owner ruled is the source of truth for the material, "in the present tense, organized by the four rollout stages" (`LANE-BRIEF-COMMON.md:106-110`). This dossier supplies the story's sources and quotations; a statement about what the engine does still traces to S5 or S2.
2. **Names.** The voice rules still forbid customer, prospect and competitor names (`LANE-BRIEF-COMMON.md:60-62`). The owner's later ruling allows internal material to "name customers and competitors as proof points (Tapestry, QVC, the Dynamic Yield displacement)", and "anything that leaves the company stays customer-neutral" (`:112-114`). The two lines need the lead's reconciliation before a writer names anyone.
3. **The working notes forbid their own reuse:** "Never send it, quote it, or paraphrase it outside the team." (`docs/Tapestry-BTIE-Working-Notes.md:3`). The requirements extraction was "Prepared for: Agentic engineering team" (`docs/architecture/tapestry-requirements-extraction-2026-09-18.md:4`). Quoting either needs the owner's ruling, however the names question is settled.
4. **Performance.** A claim is "a realistic range stated as typical" from our own measurements, with its basis in S5 §1.1, and "the exact sentence every piece uses is set by the lead in the design document" (`LANE-BRIEF-COMMON.md:29`). Any other figure in a source below, the deck's included, is listed so writers know to drop it (S5 §4 item 3; §5 Q4).
5. **Gender neutral, quotations included.** "No piece, page, figure or Word copy matches the pattern `\b(she|her|hers|he|him|his)\b`, in any case, quoted lines and never-say tables included" (`docs/enablement/BTI-Enablement-Design.md:569`). Nearly every demo script and customer quotation below says "she" or "her"; a retelling uses "the visitor" and "they", and a quoted line with a pronoun cannot be used as it stands.
6. **Wording.** Say "calls no AI model to decide"; never "we don't use a model" (S2 `:123-125`; working notes `:46`). Never "BTIE" (`LANE-BRIEF-COMMON.md:17`).

Signals that start a test or move a campaign belong to Optimizely's experimentation products and Opal beside the engine; the repository shows them only in Generation 1 demos.

## 2. The engine organizes the page by interest and decides what fills each slot

The design makes one distinction the owner asked for: with one reading of the visitor's interests the engine decides the order of a page's sections (a layout slot whose items are its modules) and the pieces inside each slot. Ordering modules inside a slot is built; the page's layout as a slot with starting orders by arrival context, module events, position correction and pins at any position is the current design's first build (P01 to P05); content inside each module follows (P26). Where each region sits on the page stays the site's own template.
