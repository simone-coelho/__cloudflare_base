## Summary

- **BTIE** is "Behavioral Targeting & Intelligence Engine", the title of Tapestry's own white paper, "VERSION 2.0", January 2026 (`docs/architecture/tapestry_requirements.txt:2-12`, `:57`). **BTI** is "Behavioral Targeting and Intelligence" (`docs/architecture/tapestry-requirements-extraction-2026-09-18.md:66`), which the paper credits to Avinash Kaushik's "BTI Manifesto" (`tapestry_requirements.txt:23`, `:406`). Our customer documents use BTI as the initiative and offering name; BTIE appears only where we cite their paper.
- **Recommended package name: Behavioral Targeting and Intelligence**, written in full, with "the engine" as the noun inside the package and one rule line on its cover. It is the only name with commercial standing, its reason ("not site personalization": the anonymous 90 percent) is the thesis every successful piece leads with, and its two halves describe the mechanism. Five conditions are in §1.8; the first is that marketing owns the final choice.
- **The five design rules I am most confident of** (§2.5, rules 1 to 5): one reader per piece, named at the top; one rule first, repeated last; organise by the shopper's session, before, during and after, with a verdict on every row in one colour language; give the sentences to say and the sentences never to say; examples before mechanism, and every instrument explained before it is shown.

**Three attributions, one set of words.** The extraction calls the term Nitin's (he used it at the start of the session); the AE validation says Mandeep chose it; the paper credits Avinash Kaushik. The working notes record Mandeep Bhatia as "Author of the BTI white paper" and Nitin Tyagi as "Co-author" (`docs/Tapestry-BTIE-Working-Notes.md` §1.2). The spelling varies only in their material: the paper writes "&", the manifesto citation "Behavior Targeting". Every Optimizely document writes "Behavioral Targeting and Intelligence".

**Why the two halves fit the engine** (my reading, not a statement in the documents). The paper's BT is the starting rules a team writes, and its I is learning that "may discover our initial rules were wrong" (`:69`). The engine has both, in that order: "'Paid social, first visit: the category grid first' is a rule a merchandiser writes, and it is in force the moment it is saved. Learning then refines it per context and reports where the evidence disagrees with the rule." (`docs/BTI-Parity-and-Mechanism.md` §3.8). What differs is the meaning of "Intelligence": the paper describes it as AI ("Manual segmentation rules" becoming "ML-driven behavior targeting", `:140`; "AI-powered intelligence", `:90`), while the engine's learning is counted arithmetic behind a dial ("Every learned number is a count", Outcome Learning §1). The package has to define the word in our terms.

### 1.3 What the engine is, in the documents' own words

Grouped by the job each sentence does. These are the sentences the package can quote.

- **The premise.** "This initiative is about the other 90 percent." and "The distinction is not branding. It is the entire addressable population." (`docs/Tapestry-Capability-Breakdown-v2.md:21`). In the session: "it's not just about content. It's about the behavior and intelligence." (Simone, extraction `:71`).
- **The model.** "one behavioural model of the shopper, with content, experience and functional units as consumers of it" (`docs/BTI-Parity-and-Mechanism.md:3`); "The behavioural model is the product." (extraction `:74`, an annotation).
- **The question it answers.** "of the candidates you have registered, which one belongs here, for this shopper, right now, and why." (`docs/BTI-Implementation-Guide.md:40`).
- **The seam.** "Your pages send events. We return decisions addressed by your own identifiers. Your front end renders, always, and renders your default when there is no decision." (`docs/BTI-Implementation-One-Pager.md:1`); "we run the engine, the environments and the evidence" (working notes `:115`).
- **The control.** "Gates, then pins, then weighted ranking. Your rules decide what can and must show. Affinity decides what does show in the space that is left." (guide `:82`); "Merchandising outranks the engine" and "Your page is the baseline" (`docs/BTI-Phase-1-Foundation.md:282-283`).
- **The mechanism in one line.** "An ordering is a ranking." (parity `:55`); "The unit of state is the dimension value, not the click" (`public/vantage/how-it-works.html` §1).
- **The honesty line.** "No model is called at decision time." (parity `:264`); "It is deterministic at decision time." (guide `:45`).
- **The architecture in one line.** "ODP is the memory; the edge is the reflex." (`docs/GTM-Delivery-Playbook.md:13`).
- **The category.** "the decision and delivery platform that acts on signals, governs them, explains them and delivers by identifier" (working notes `:391`); "Content is treated as a product." (`:398`); "They score people; we decide pages." (`docs/Quin-vs-Platform-At-a-Glance.md:82`).
- **The entry analogy** (the July and August material). "We recommend content the way we recommend products." with "If you understand product recommendations ... you already understand most of this." (Solution and Algorithm masthead; `docs/Content-Personalization-Explained-Simply.md` §1). It matches the customer's own framing, "Three Types of Recommendations" (product, content, experience) with "Product recommendations are table stakes" (`tapestry_requirements.txt:233-239`), and Nitin's "we feel the same ranking approach will work for content as well" (extraction, quotations, ≈25:10).

**On recommendations, precisely.** "Never a recommendations engine" holds as positioning: complementary to product recommendations, which the customer itself calls table stakes. But the July and August material used recommendations deliberately as the way in ("We recommend content the way we recommend products"), and the customer's own paper calls what it wants a "Content & Experience Recommendation System" (`tapestry_requirements.txt:240`). The Opticon collapse shows the risk of stopping at the analogy. The package can use it once, as the entry, and then state the difference in the same paragraph: the engine orders the page and fills the other modules, for a shopper nobody has identified, and writes down why.

**What the documents do not say.** None calls another system "legacy". The rule runs the other way: the Verizon set holds that "we explain our own reasoning and never describe or judge what the customer has built" (`docs/verizon/Vantage-Explainer-Share-Notes.md`); the SOW input "makes no assumption about what Tapestry runs today" (Deliverables, Not included); and the Quin notes forbid saying "that their intent model is weak" (working notes `:400`). The contrast is drawn against mechanisms (buckets assigned overnight, pre-built variations, rules someone wrote, a model nobody can inspect), never against a product or the customer's own stack.

**Two sentences never to say.** "We do not use a model", which "undersells the engine to exactly the audience that reads these" (working notes §0, `:46`); and "The edge signals are probabilistic", which "hands back the black box that the whole no-black-box argument removes" (AE Answer Pack `:163`). The Solution and Algorithm document's §05 heading still reads "Deterministic in computation, probabilistic in interpretation"; that document is in the customer's hands, and position 3 already plans to revise one of its sentences with them.

### 1.8 Recommendation: one name, and what to retire

**Name the package and the offering "Behavioral Targeting and Intelligence".** Write it in full in anything a customer or a marketer reads, use BTI only as an internal short form, call the thing itself "the engine" inside the package (as the Opal page, the parity document and the operator console already do), and never use BTIE. Keep the US spelling in the name, which is the contract's and the customer's; the spelling of the prose around it is a house-style choice (the recent documents write British).

Why this name:

1. **It is the only name with commercial standing.** The AE validation says "The contract calls it **Behavioral Targeting and Intelligence**" (`docs/Tapestry-AE-Capabilities-Doc-Validation.md:73`; also the CFO review, `:210`). It heads the guide and the one-pager the customer holds (the guide's title, the one-pager's subtitle). The Solution and Algorithm document adopted it "deliberately". Optimizely's product and commercial lead took it "under advisement" as the product name, and the owner endorsed it (extraction `:66`).
2. **Its reason is the thesis that sells.** "That is why the initiative is named Behavioral Targeting and Intelligence rather than site personalization. The distinction is not branding. It is the entire addressable population." (Capability Breakdown v2 `:21`). The Opticon session opened on the same point: "Who you are actually personalizing for" (`docs/opticon/Opticon-Demo-Design.md`, Act 0).
3. **Its two halves describe the mechanism** (§1.1): the rules and starting orders a team writes, then learning that refines them behind a dial.
4. **It names neither a channel nor an infrastructure.** "Content decision service" and "Content Personalization on the Edge Affinity Engine" each describe one consumer of the model, and experience now ships first. The parity document's premise is "one behavioural model of the shopper, with content, experience and functional units as consumers of it".

Conditions:

1. **Marketing owns the final choice.** "Marketing owns naming/packaging — this doc deliberately avoids branding the offering so that choice stays open." (GTM playbook `:41`). Present it in the package as the working name, with the decision recorded.
2. **Keep it apart from the behavioral targeting Optimizely already sells.** The CFO capability document lists "**Behavioral targeting** on on-site actions, journey stage, and engagement" among the platform capabilities "available today" (Capability Breakdown v2 `:50`). Always use the four words and the anonymous-first reason, so an account executive never hears "we already sell behavioral targeting".
3. **In other customers' material, never present the name as Tapestry's**, and never cite the white paper or Avinash Kaushik's manifesto. BTIE is their paper's name for the system they set out to build.
4. **Define "Intelligence" as counted learning behind a dial**, not as AI: "Every learned number is a count" and "Nothing changes until someone says so" (Outcome Learning §1). The Opal page's answer to "If the engine is so smart, why doesn't it use AI credits?" is the existing answer to "Is it AI?".
5. **Confirm the order-form line name before building the order-form map.** The Order Form appendix drafts v7 and v8 are titled "Experience & Content Personalization", while the AE validation says the contract calls the program BTI. The executed wording is not in the repository.

### 2.6 What to avoid

1. **The point after a long sequence.** Outcome Learning puts its rollout, and its one leadership sentence, at §16 of 0 to 17. The Solution and Algorithm document puts the parts at §10, the stages at §14 and "In one breath" after §18. The primer reaches its destination on screen 9 of 9. The test: if the point is not on the first screen, the reader will not get to it.
2. **Terms that are never defined, even on the good page.** Opal: "CMAB serving decisions", "OptiAnalytics", "Discovery proposals (stage 3)". Outcome Learning: `waitUntil`, "Durable Object · SQLite", "Analytics Engine". The primer: K, τ and R in the notes of its second screen.
3. **Instruments before explanation.** The primer, on every screen; compare the how-it-works page after the owner's round (rule 5).
4. **One page for several readers.** See rule 1.
5. **Caveats on every screen.** See rule 8.
6. **Interactive state that carries across screens.** "One visitor, nine screens. The state carries through the story." A reader who opens a screen out of order sees nothing happen.
7. **Numbers that differ from the engine's, and claims the rulings withdrew.** The primer's K = 3 and its memories, against K = 1.8 (`src/reflex/core.ts:260`); "all in under 50ms" and "single-digit-millisecond decision time worldwide" (SA guide), "<50ms" (Revenue Radar), against position 25; "First paint is served from a snapshot, so nothing flashes" and "the new decision is pushed to the page that is already open" (Deliverables), against positions 9 and 10; the 40 to 60 percent target, against position 22.
8. **A stale structure.** "rungs cannot be skipped" (GTM §2), "Customers can't skip steps" (Explained Simply §7), layout at "~6 months" (Solution and Algorithm §14, primer 03): all reversed when experience moved first (positions 13 and 19; parity §3.9, "Does it depend on content personalization coming first? No.").
9. **Several names for one thing** (§1.7). The account team was told so in August: "This is now the third name for the same program".
10. **Density in the demo that the package points to.** The Coach demo "lost the room on **density, not quality**" (BOOTSTRAP §3); the owner's remedy: "Legibility over density. Nothing changes without a press." (BOOTSTRAP §12, item 5). And from the GTM playbook: "This demo closes rooms — lead with it, not slides."
