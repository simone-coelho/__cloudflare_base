Slide 2 · what they already have

What a recommendation engine typically does

It answers one very important question: “Which products should we put in front of this shopper?”

Input signals
History + behaviour + product relationships
Past browsing, purchases, item similarity, popularity, collaborative patterns, and sometimes current session activity.
↓
Decision engine
Recommendation logic
Runs one or more algorithms to pick and rank products.
↓
Output
A ranked product list
For example: “You may also like”, “Complete the look”, “Recommended for you”.
This is valuable. We do not need to replace it to add value.

The simplest way to say it

A recommendation engine is primarily about which products to show and what order to show them in.

Slide 3 · what Optimizely adds

What Optimizely does differently

We track the interests behind the shopper’s actions, then use those changing interests to decide what the experience should show next.

Observe
Read what the shopper is doing now
Views, saves, add-to-bag events, page context and other approved signals.
↓
Interpret
Build live interest signals
“Wedding guest”, “occasionwear”, “everyday knitwear”, “premium”, “sale seeker” — whatever dimensions you decide to track.
↓
Act
Choose the experience around the products
Lead image, story, gallery order, campaign module, supporting content, and other approved placements.
This is not just “moving blocks around”. It is making live content decisions from current shopper intent.

The simplest way to say it

Optimizely is about how the products are presented, which supporting content is shown, and how quickly the experience reacts when the shopper changes direction.

Recommended phrase

“Your recommender finds products. We make the experience around those products respond to the shopper in the moment.”

Important nuance

Optimizely can also rank candidates itself — but the strongest entry point is not replacement. The strongest entry point is complementary value.
Leave-behind line: “We turn behaviour into live experience decisions.”
