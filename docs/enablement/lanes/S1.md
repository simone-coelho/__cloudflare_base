## 3. The contract for the page lanes

1. **Markers, exact forms.** Right after `<body>`: `<!-- chrome:header:start --><!-- chrome:header:end -->`. Inside
   the `<article>`, right after the h1, before any section or heading of the page:
   `<!-- spine:start --><!-- spine:end -->`; on the battlecard
   `<!-- spine:compact:start --><!-- spine:compact:end -->` instead. After `</main>`:
   `<!-- chrome:footer:start --><!-- chrome:footer:end -->`. Write them empty, then run
   `node enablement/render-pages.mjs --stamp`. Never edit inside a stamped block. Re-stamp after saving a page whole,
   because a save from an unstamped copy empties the blocks again.
2. **What the stamp does.** It fills each pair from `enablement/partials/`, marks the page's own link in the
   navigation and the footer with `aria-current="page"`, keeps the page's line endings, writes a page only when it
   changes, reports a page without markers as skipped, and writes nothing else into a page. It also brings the figure
   copies in `enablement/site/figures/` up to `docs/enablement/figures/`, byte for byte, because page lanes do not
   run the plain builder. It is idempotent: a second run reports "already current" everywhere and changes no byte
   (checked by checksum).
3. **The head**, each tag at most once, in exactly these forms: `<link rel="stylesheet" href="/site.css">`,
   `<link rel="stylesheet" href="/spine.css">`, `<link rel="stylesheet" href="/<name>.css">`,
   `<script src="/site.js" defer></script>`, `<script src="/spine.js" defer></script>`,
   `<script src="/<name>.js" defer></script>`. A page with the full spine loads `/spine.css` and `/spine.js`; the
   battlecard needs only `/spine.css`.
4. **The article** names its two texts of record in this order:
   `data-source="docs/enablement/content/spine.md docs/enablement/content/<name>.md"`. The how-it-works page names
   `how-it-works-and-where-it-runs.md`. The battlecard names
   `docs/enablement/content/spine-compact.md docs/enablement/content/battlecard.md`, and its compact block is compared
   with spine-compact.md. The page's markdown holds the page's visible text in order, not the spine's. A spine
   sentence repeated in the page's own sections is compared with the page's markdown.
5. **`data-visual`**: every `<section>` holds an element carrying it (or is one), and that element draws: it is, or
   holds, an svg, a table, an img or a figure, or an element with no text that the stylesheet draws. A div of text
   alone is not a visual (review round four, section 9).
6. **`data-shot`**: on what-it-is, see-it-work, selling-it and battlecard, every `<section>` holds one. The value is
   the name without the prefix. `data-shot="ae-scenes"` pairs with `![caption](../figures/shot-ae-scenes.png)` in the
   markdown, and the element's text must contain the caption. A shot PNG not yet captured is listed, never failed.
7. **`data-reader`** marks what speaks to the reader as reader. Outside it, no "you", "your", "yours" or "yourself",
   in text, inline SVG text, accessible names or the document title. A page's own script must not hold those words
   in any string it can write, because the check cannot see where a script puts text. Since round four it sits only
   on leaf blocks (a paragraph, a heading, a list item or cell with no block inside), never on a section, the
   article or any container, and no more than half of the article's words may sit under it (section 9).
   **`data-ui-label`** (the lead's ruling, 2026-10-03) marks a quoted interface label addressed to the shopper, not
   the reader, such as a carousel's own title "Recommended for you" in a pictured page. Its text is the one other
   thing the reader scan leaves out. It is still compared with the markdown and scanned for everything else, and it
   holds at most six words: a label, never a sentence. Put it on the label itself, not on the line around it.
8. **The prose budget.** No block element's own text over 60 words on what-it-is, see-it-work, selling-it and
   battlecard, or over 120 words on how-it-works. That covers paragraphs, headings, list items, cells, figures,
   sections and the rest, so text in a heading or in a span inside a figure counts, and so does each SVG text
   element. Code in `<pre>` is not prose.
9. **Class names and labels.** On the five pages, no class may contain the part `chip`, `label` or `legend`,
   singular or plural (`x-label` and `chips` are refused). On every page, no element may read only a label word
   (Open, Built, Designed, Coming soon, Beta, Preview).
10. **Reserved.** Ids starting with `spine-`, the classes `spine*` and `.mark` inside `.spine`. Scope page CSS to the
    page's own markup, never bare `section`, `h2`, `p` or `.mark`. No lane's stylesheet reaches into the spine today
    (checked by search and by screenshots of each page's spine).
11. **The stamp line** on every page is "Updated October 3, 2026".

## 7. Where I departed from V3 or my brief, and why

The lead ruled on every item below on 2026-10-03 (section 8): item 1 is reversed, the others accepted.

1. **"Recommended for you" became "Product row"** in the two depicted carousel titles, because my brief said the
   spine has no "you" or "your" at all and V3's reader scan fails "you" outside `[data-reader]`. **Reversed by the
   lead's ruling:** the title is restored and marked `data-ui-label` (section 8).
2. **The loop plays once, about six seconds, and holds its end state** instead of repeating forever. "Play again"
   restarts it, and spine.js plays it the first time it comes into view. Movement ends within four seconds, under
   WCAG 2.2.2's five-second line for motion that has no pause control. "Play again" would mean nothing on an endless
   loop, and the gate (V3 section 7.5) asks that it "runs and replays". The bucket frame likewise fills and drains
   three times, about 4.2 seconds, and holds.
3. **The trade of places is a true trade:** boots to first, hero to second, as V3 words it. Mock 2's keyframes moved
   the hero down two places.
4. **The short form's "three contrast lines"** are one row per contrast: the other system and its line, then "The
   engine" and its line. The two fixed sentences are left to the battlecard's own block of four.
5. **The walk-through** is an `h3` inside block 4's section on the page, so the spine stays four sections, and a `##`
   in spine.md, because the Word converter uses only `#` and `##`.
6. **`data-shot` values follow V3 section 5's formula:** `data-shot="spine-story"` pairs with `shot-spine-story.png`.
   My brief listed the names as "shot-spine-story" and so on, which are the PNG names.
7. **"different module"** sits beside the story region, a module the engine chose, so each mark points at a region:
   lead image, story, product row.

player. It allows:
- the stamp line beside the reader tag;

- one intro line, the first `p` with text after the title;

- comments, scripts, styles, templates and line breaks, which render nothing.

Inside the title and the intro line, only phrasing elements that hold text may appear. Everything else fails, filed
under T1, and the finding names what it found:
- an element with text, hidden or not, since a script may show it;
- bare text;
- a link, including one inside the title or the intro line;
- a picture (`img`, `svg`, `picture`, `figure`, `canvas`, video, audio, a frame, `role="img"`);
- a drawn element (`data-visual` or `data-shot`);
- a control or a heading;
