## 3. Choices, and why

- **The rule.** Built on the thesis that sold (S3 §1.3, §1.8 item 2: "the entire addressable population") and on the marketing rule in `what-it-is.md`, which the coordinator reports is being realigned to "With consent, the engine remembers what each visitor is interested in, not who they are, and ranks your content by the rules your team publishes." The AE rule keeps its "with consent" and its "interest, not who they are" ("by interest, not identity"), adds the thesis ("the entire addressable population from the first click") and closes on the account executive's own discipline ("claim only what is Built"). "Why it sells" states the "not site personalization for the known few" half in full. The coordinator confirmed the first clause stays "by interest, not identity", which is already neutral.
- **Gender neutral, always** (owner's ruling, now in the common brief's voice section). The 20 gendered pronouns in the first accepted text sat on eight lines. They were in the one-sentence description, the session sentence above the claims table, three claims rows, the Vantage and storefront demo rows, and the click-tracking objection. All now read "the visitor" or "they, them, their", and no line mixes a role noun with a gendered pronoun. Two sentences were also reworded for clarity. The regional prior row now says "other visitors in the same region", so that "their region" cannot be read as the other visitors'. The never-say sentence now reads "It remembers every click a visitor makes", because it stands without an antecedent.
- **The glossary is adopted.** It arrived after the first draft, so the piece was revised to it: visitor rather than shopper (kept only in the credits wording "shopper traffic" and the line name "Shopper-facing AI features", as the marketing piece also does), off limits for the buy box, step rather than phase for the implementation path, holdout as "a share of visitors held back for comparison" (both arms), autonomy as "switched off by policy and never offered", exploration as open. A scan finds none of the glossary's deprecated words.
- **The title** follows the siblings' pattern, "Behavioral Targeting and Intelligence: selling it". The name appears once in the source, in the title; "See also" names the siblings by their subtitles so the name is not repeated.
- **The claims table has the brief's eleven claims, two more from S2 §10.1 and one from S2 §10.4.** The two from §10.1 are claims account executives make: product re-ranking (Built) and native mobile apps (Open, "never sell it as available"). The SE piece labels both the same way, and the coordinator kept them. The row from §10.4 is the module-order fact, below.
- **Module order: one added row, at the coordinator's direction.** Before S2's §10.4 the piece had no line about module order, by design: S2 §10.1 then said only "Designed", while BOOTSTRAP §3 showed modules reordering. So the one line the fact affects is a new claims row, placed during the visit, next to product re-ranking. Its claim is the one account executives make after the Vantage phone page: "The page rearranges itself for each visitor". It is labelled **Designed**, because the claim as stated is the layout changing, and per-context layout configuration is Designed (§10.4 item 3). Its sentence is S2's approved sentence, verbatim (§10.4 item 4). That sentence states the Built part (§10.4 item 1: the engine orders whatever sits in a slot, including the phone page's `pdp-modules`). It also keeps each slot's place on the page with the site's template, which on the demo homepages is the page's own script (Demo only, §10.4 item 2). It carries no pronoun. The demo table, the Vantage caution and the implementation path's "Designed, the first build item" line are unchanged, and they stay true under §10.4.
- **"It learns automatically" is labelled Open**, on decision D05 (which exploration a customer may switch on, S2 §10.3). Its sentence says the engine counts outcomes, moves a ranking only as far as the learning dial allows, and never changes its own weights, which is switched off by policy and never offered. That matches the glossary and the SE piece, which give withdrawn features no label. "Sub-second decisions" appears only as the claim to refuse; the piece quotes no figure.
- **Receipts and learning are Built with the one exception.** The common brief's fact that the shipped SDK records nothing yet is the piece's single flagged exception, as in the marketing piece. Each row that depends on it links to it, and the "Can we see why it decided?" answer says "once recording is on".
- **Headings.** The section headings are the lead's quoted names, verbatim. The coordinator ruled them fine for this release; the reviewer judges consistency across the three pieces.
- **Demos: six, at the coordinator's direction.** The Bright Hour was added as the fourth row, with the caution that it runs on the shared demo deployment's older build. The intro now reads "Six hosted demos", and its older-build sentence names The Bright Hour. Its row is from BOOTSTRAP §3 and §5.3 (decisions under expiry). It omits that demo's AI tagging and experiment beats, which belong to the older pipeline. The storefront is named without its brand, by its URL. Its caution is BOOTSTRAP §6's imagery rule, and the bank demo's is BOOTSTRAP §5.2 (its beats swap from a table in the page). Meridian's is BOOTSTRAP §5.4's wording for the hands-off exit.
- **The implementation path's two tellings** come from S1 §2.2 and §4 item 7, as the brief directs, and are stated as open. The delivery team is named "a specialist delivery team (SWAT)".
- **The customer table** has the brief's four asks plus the registry and retention periods, each with who provides it (S2 §7.7; the Vantage note's shape). One line hands the remaining technical items to the SE.
- **The credits page title** is written as the marketing piece writes it, *Where Opal and AI credits fit, and where they never do*, without the source's em dash.
