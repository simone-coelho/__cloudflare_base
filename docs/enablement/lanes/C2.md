## 4. Choices, and why

- **Not one build-state word.** The piece avoids "designed", "today", "open", "stage", "not yet", "later", "labels" and "built" altogether, even in harmless senses ("thin labels", "build an audience", "open the console", "open a demo"), so no scan of the piece can find one. The same goes for verdicts, rollout order and any deal won for a reason.
- **Quotations by industry only, none with a pronoun.**
  - "It works, but it caps out." and "a site that feels like a stylist who remembers" come from a luxury retailer's own brief (S4 rows 8.32 and 8.33).
  - "How else would it learn? I don't understand." is a program lead at a luxury retailer (S4 row 8.13), answered with the design's own sentence, "An ordering is a ranking." (S4 row 2.3).
  - The stylist line in the same brief carries an em-dash, so the piece uses its dash-free twin rather than altering a quotation.
  - The customer's figures on anonymous traffic are not used: the brief asks for no other figure, and S4 calls them one customer's numbers.
  - **The luxury retailer's conclusion about the market goes in by industry, on the coordinator's ruling**, in the ruling's own words: "One of our customers in luxury retail concluded that this capability did not exist off the shelf, and built it with us." It sits in the stylist section, right after the brief's quotation.
    - Its basis is the same customer's paper, "This capability doesn't exist off the shelf; we need to build it." (S4 row 8.30), and the signature (S4 row 7.8).
    - It is the piece's one "built", sanctioned by the ruling, and it speaks of the customer's choice, not of build state. The scans set that one sentence aside and still find no build-state word elsewhere.
- **Two words the glossary deprecates are V2's own.** "Buckets" is the brief's device for interest (§2 item 3), and "placements they sell" is its reason (§2 item 4), so the brief governs both. "In real time" is the brief's phrase too.
- **The architecture figure is back, redrawn.** Lane D1 redrew it under V2 (`74ec6a1`: no legend, no labels, no numbers). It now sits where the story speaks of the edge, right after "The stranger from an ad", as `![Where the engine runs on Cloudflare](../figures/architecture.png)`.
  - The lead line above it says only what the drawing shows: the site talking to the engine on Cloudflare's network, close to the visitor; the brand's team in the console, with Opal working beside them; and the links to ODP, Feature Experimentation and the brand's own warehouse.
  - The drawing's own text was checked first. Its only near miss is "backstage" in Opal's box, which is not build state; the lead line says "working beside them" instead, so the piece's scans stay at zero.
  - The earlier drawing, with its "Open" tags and legend, was left out until this redraw.
- **Social signals and the partner quiz are worded as S5 §5 Q6 recommends.** The link from a social post or a partner's quiz carries a campaign term that seeds the first page. That keeps the brief's channels while saying what the engine reads.
- **Opal launching tests on signals is told as the brief tells it:** Opal proposes a test on a trending signal and launches it through Feature Experimentation, contextual bandit tests included, with a person approving. The engine's own decision calls no AI model.
- **The mobile objection** is answered as "It runs on the website, through one script the site adds once", with "It runs in your native app" as the never-say (S5 B9; §4 item 9).
- **Demos.**
  - Vantage, its explainer, Top Offers, Meridian, The Bright Hour, OH and the storefront each show named scenes.
  - The storefront is marked "for internal viewing only", because it carries one brand's own products.
  - The bank demo is left out, because it shows an earlier platform rather than any scene in this story.
  - OH has been hosted since `6484544`, and BOOTSTRAP §9 lists it. Its row, added at the coordinator's direction, calls it a home-appliances store made for one prospect, with "Ask the demo owner before showing it".
    - Its scenes come from the presenter script's sixteen beats: the stranger from a search ad onto a regional first page; interest moving across rooms, then fading over a marked jump of several weeks; a closing offer window and a pinned launch; sign-in carrying a visitor from laptop to phone; pieces that complete the product; partner pickups and retailer referrals credited to their modules.
    - "Made for" stands in for the coordinator's "built for", so the piece stays free of the word.
    - The prospect's name, the retail partners and the fictional account's name stay out.
- **The outcomes and questions** follow the deck's six topics in our own words. The deck's "under 50 ms" and its computed "cost of an overridden campaign" are left out: the fixed performance sentence is the only figure, and S5 §5 Q7 finds no computed cost per override. The placements outcome counts collisions instead.
- **Links:** the credits page, the solutions engineers' piece and the demo map, each with its title as visible text and its full address as target. The demos carry their own addresses.
