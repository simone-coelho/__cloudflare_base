**Recommendations.**
- **R-c28:** the column header is "Verdict".
- **R-c29:** slot and piece are defined in the example, which is their first use.

**Adjusted, with the reason.**
1. **R-c3's sentence is a sentence to say.** The line it replaced, "Why it sells", has no place among §3.4's sections, so its replacement sentence became a sentence to say. It is true to S2 §10.1 and §3.1, and design §3.3 gives (b) a near twin.
2. **R-c24 says "the brand's operators" for "your operators",** so the question section keeps one voice: the design's answers speak of the brand and the customer in the third person.
3. **R-c12's second sentence moved to the never-say column.** The review's answer for "Does it learn on its own?" ends "The engine changing its own weights is switched off by policy and never offered." Design §3.1 ("Never rows") and §4.2 name withdrawn things only on the never-say list. So the answer keeps the first sentence, and the never-say row carries §4.9's wording, "Automatic weight changes and Thompson sampling are switched off by policy and not offered."
4. **R-c14 says "the Vantage and Top Offers demos" where it said "the hosted demos".** S2 §3.15 names those two pages as the ones that record. Four other hosted demos run the older pipeline (S2 §2.6), so "the hosted demos" would claim more than S2 holds.
5. **"Who sets what": windows sit with the API, not the console.** Design §3.4 item 6 lists "windows" in the console row. S2 §4.5 says catalog content has no console screen, and a piece's time window and eligibility rule are catalog fields (S2 §4.2). So they sit in the API row, and the console row says "rules" for the slot rules its screen does carry.

**Declined, with the reason.**
1. **P8 is not in the sentences to say.** Design §3.4's list of sentences to say omits it, although the P table lists P8 for (c). Its first sentence is the figure's third sentence in other words, and its open items are rows B9, B11, B12 and A13. Add it back if the lead prefers the P table.
2. **No `content/demo-map.md` and no change to `content/credits-source.html`,** although design §9 names C2 as their owner. This lane's brief names two paths only. The demo map already exists as `content/demos.md`, written by another lane, and carries this lane's cautions.
3. **No session row is reworded by this lane.** B7 first read "Every setting is a published, versioned document", which has the same flaw the review found in R-c25: worker settings need an engineer (S2 §4.6). The piece kept it verbatim, as §3.1 requires, and flagged it. The lead has since reworded the master row (verdict Built), and the piece mirrors the new row verbatim: "Every rule and weight the brand sets is a published, versioned document, in force without a software release, with its history and a rollback that is itself a new revision; identity and access settings are worker configuration set by an engineer."

**Length (S-c6).** At 3,252 words, the piece is over §3.4's budget of 1,800 to 2,400. The design's own content accounts for most of it: 35 rows, eleven asks, twelve answers, P1, P2 and the never-say ledger. On top of that come the five objections the coordinator asked to carry over, about 180 words, and the never-say rows those objections bring, about 110. The cheapest cuts are those five questions, the never-say rows beyond §3.4's four and the coordinator's named items, and the five-sentence figure lead (rule 5 asks for a lead above every figure, so a one-line lead would have to replace it). The lead decides.
