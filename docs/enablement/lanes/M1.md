## The page, in the order V2 §2 sets

1. **The problem**: "Most visitors are strangers", one paragraph.
2. **What it is**: the stylist (reads the room, watches without interrogating, rearranges the table, pivots), with
   the fixed recommendations sentence and the sentence that it replaces no recommendations product. It introduces
   the sample store, Morrow & Vale, a fictional brand of clothes and home goods, and says its products and regional
   leans are invented.
3. **What it does**, as four scenes, each explained in plain words above its instrument, then shown:
   - **The first page already feels local.** A two-button switch, Washington State or Florida. The store's hero and
     its five sections reorder for a stranger from each region; a panel shows what visitors in that region lean
     toward (bars, no numbers); the receipt says why ("Visitors in Florida lean toward linen, then tableware, then
     rain layers"), and that sections nothing points to keep the brand's own order. The region chosen here carries
     into the other three scenes, which reset to it.
   - **The first click changes the page.** Every product and story card is a button. A click fills the buckets of
     the card's tags (a napkin set fills tableware and linen), the "What the page leans on" bar shifts from the
     region toward the visitor's own clicks, then the visitor's next page rearranges: whole sections move, the hero
     can change, and the recommendations carousel is placed like any section. The receipt records each click, each
     move ("The linen shop moved up, ahead of the partner spotlight and the recommendations carousel"), the entry
     when a bucket passes its entry line, and the cap when extra clicks add less. The fixed performance sentence sits
     under this scene.
   - **Relevance expires on its own.** The visitor spent the morning on one kind of thing (knitwear in Washington
     State, tableware in Florida), past the entry line. Start the clock (or Step forward): the bucket drains on the
     engine's arithmetic, the visitor is released when it falls below the release line, recorded on the receipt as
     an event ("Released from knitwear: the bucket drained below its release line. Nobody wrote a rule; time did
     it."), and the page settles back to the stranger's regional first page, when the clock stops with "Settled".
     A clock face and a row of day dots show time passing without numbers. The fixed learning sentence sits under
     this scene, after "Fading interest is one thing, and learning what works is another."
   - **The merchandiser keeps the wheel.** Toggles to pin the partner spotlight (a sold section) first and to set a
     gate that hides anything out of stock, and a switch for what the visitor is into. The pinned section holds the
     first place while the others reorder around it; the out-of-stock jacket, which interest ranks first in its
     section, never appears. The receipt lists the gate, the pin ("Ranking was skipped for it: an instruction, not
     a decision. Interest alone would have placed it fourth.") and the ranking, then two lists side by side: "The
     page shows" and "Interest alone would rank".
4. **The stylist has more moves**: the capabilities the scenes do not show, one line each (channels seed the visit;
   every setting in the open; Opal launches tests on signals in Feature Experimentation once a person approves;
   identity across sign-in; decisions in the brand's warehouse). Added so the page leaves none of V2 §2 item 3 out;
   the lead can drop it without touching anything else.
5. **Why customers choose it**: all ten reasons from V2 §2 item 4 as equal cards, introduced with "Each customer
   weighs these reasons differently. In no particular order:". The Opal card ("Opal authors audiences and tests,
   explains results and tags content, backstage.") carries the fixed AI sentence beside its text.
6. Two links: "Read the whole story" (`/what-it-is`: the problem, every capability, what a customer provides) and
   "See the arithmetic" (`/how-it-works-interactive`: how it works, for engineers). How it works and what a customer

## The arithmetic behind the instruments

All of it is in one block of `see-it-work.js` between `/* model:start */` and `/* model:end */`; the still pictures
were drawn from that same block, so they cannot disagree with the live instruments.

| Term | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Interest | a = R/(R + K), K = 1.8 | FACTS §1 |
| Fading | R·e^(−Δt/τ), τ = 14 days (the engine's default memory) | FACTS §1 |
| Lines | enter at a ≥ 0.60 on an event, leave below 0.45; time alone only releases | FACTS §1 |
| Touch | each click adds weight 1 to every tag of the card (a product view or a content click) | FACTS §1 |
| Regional blend | λ = kBlend/(kBlend + Σa); blended = (1 − λ)·a + λ·share | FACTS §3 |
| kBlend | 0.5, the published value FACTS §3 cites; the compiled default is 1 | FACTS §3 |
| Score | Σ over the slot's dimensions of weight × the sum of the blended interests of the piece's tags; one dimension, weight 1 | FACTS §2 |
| Order | gates, then pins, then score; ties in the brand's own order | FACTS §2 |

Why kBlend 0.5: at 1, a stranger's first click almost never moves a section (the region keeps about three quarters
of the blend), so "the first click changes the page" would not show; at the published 0.5 a click on knitwear in
Washington State takes the top, and a click on a value the region ignores lifts its section above the carousel.

Measured on the model: the morning bucket (three looks) is released on day 9.9 and the page settles back on day
15.6, at the 14-day memory; the clock runs at 1.6 days a second, so about six and ten seconds. The store's data is
invented: regional shares Washington State rain 1, knitwear 0.75, tableware 0.5, linen 0; Florida linen 1, tableware
0.75, rain 0.5, knitwear 0. The carousel carries no tag, so interest never lifts it and it sits after the sections
that match, in the brand's order; the partner spotlight is tagged tableware. None of these numbers is on the page.

## After review round three: the "negative width" console error (R.md R3.6, RR3-13)

- **The fix**, applied as the review wrote it: `see-it-work.js:203`, the easing fraction is now clamped at zero,
  `const k = Math.max(0, Math.min(1, (t - t0) / ms));`. A frame's timestamp can precede the `performance.now()` read
  when a tween starts; the fraction then went negative, the cubic ease overshot backward, and a region bar (or a
  bucket) was given a negative width. With the clamp every eased value stays between its start and its target.
- **A correction to the pass above.** Headless Chrome on this machine reports `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` by
  default, and the page then skips every tween and animation. So the first pass of 177 checks exercised only the
  reduced-motion path, which is why it never met the error the review saw. Every run below forces motion on
  (`prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference`) and asserts it in the page; a separate run asserts reduced motion.
- **Reproduced, then gone.** With motion on and every animation frame stamped 500 ms early (injected over CDP), the
  page served with the old line logged 72 "A negative value is not valid" errors over three region switches; with
  the fixed line, none, and no other error.
- **Stress at the three sizes.** At 1440, 1366 and 390 wide, light and dark, with real frames: 162 rapid changes per
  run across the four moves (region switches, card clicks, the clock started, paused, stepped and run to its end,
  pin, gate and interest switches, with random gaps of 10 to 230 ms). Every run: no console or log error, no
  rectangle with a negative or invalid width or height, no sideways overflow.
- **The full suite again, with animation live:** 190 checks, all passing (the 177 above, now run on the animated
  path, plus 12 assertions that motion is on and one that the reduced-motion run sees reduced motion). The text check (92 sentences each way, none unexplained) and the 15
  preflight checks on this page pass on the fixed file.
