## 2. The one rule, and why this one

> **The engine is a personal stylist for every visitor, even a stranger: it arranges the page around what they are doing now, and your team keeps the last word.**

Twenty-nine words, in four parts, each carrying one thing a marketer must be able to say:
- **"A personal stylist for every visitor"** is V2's own image (§2.2). It comes from a customer's paper and the deck (S4 9.3), and it says what the engine is.
- **"Even a stranger"** is the problem it solves (V2 §2.1): most visitors are anonymous, and the engine needs no history (S5 V1).
- **"Arranges the page around what they are doing now"** is what it does, and the difference from recommendations, which rank the past (V2 §2.2, §3). "Arranges" is the stylist's verb, from the move "rearranges the table".
- **"Your team keeps the last word"** is control. It is the shared primer's own line (S4 9.21), and it carries gates, pins, the console and the dial (S5 C1, C7, F7) in four words.

It names no condition: consent appears once, in the privacy answer, as V2 and the coordinator ask. Like the sentences to say, it speaks to the customer ("your team"); the body says "the brand".

I weighed two shorter forms. "Every visitor gets a personal stylist; your team keeps the last word" loses the stranger and the "now". "A personal stylist for every visitor, at internet scale" (the deck) loses control. I chose length over losing either.

## 6. Decisions and points for the lead

- **No link to the status page.** "What is built, designed and open" is a build-state page, and V2 forbids build state. See also links Selling it, How it works and where it runs, the credits page, the demo map and the glossary. If the status page is retired or rewritten under V2, a link can be added.
- **The performance sentence appears once,** in "Four sentences are always said word for word". My draft also answered "How fast is it?" with it. I dropped that, to match lane C3's once-only rule and keep the package consistent. The other three fixed sentences are each said in the story and again in the list; the learning and recommendations sentences also appear in the never-say table as say-instead lines.
- **"Much of the buying,"** not V2's "most buying". S4 8.27 holds one customer's figure, a share of purchases on the first visit, and it does not reach "most".
- **"Every weight, memory and threshold,"** not "every constant". This is S5 C7's list, and S5 §5 Q12 recommends the narrower words because some constants are fixed in code.
- **Buckets.** The glossary deprecates "bucket". V2 makes it the marketing device and replaces conflicting rules, so the piece uses it, always as the image of one interest, and never for audiences or visit counts.
- **The signal-led test** (scene 4) is told as Opal proposing a test and launching it once a person approves. That follows V2 §2.3 and S4 1.15 to 1.22. The person's approval is from S4 3.21 and S5 F14, and it carries the governance reason customers give (S4 3.11 to 3.15).
- **Rule format.** The design's §3.0 item 3 sets the rule as a bold paragraph, second and last, which this piece uses. Lane C3's checker expects a `>` callout (`> **...**`), as C3's and C2's pieces use. The lead may want one format across the pieces; the text is unaffected.
- **Shared scratchpad.** Lane C3 wrote its checker to `check-v2.mjs`, the name my first checker had, in the session's shared scratchpad. I left C3's file alone and moved mine to `c1-check-v2.mjs`.
