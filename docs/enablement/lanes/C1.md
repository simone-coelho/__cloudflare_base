## 2. The one rule, and why this one

> **With consent, the engine remembers what each visitor is interested in, not who they are, and ranks your content by the rules your team publishes.**

It keeps the candidate's core ("what a visitor is interested in, not who they are", and the team's published rules) and makes two changes:

1. **"With consent" leads.** S2 §1 corrects the very phrase the candidate rests on: "for each visitor" becomes "for each visitor who has consented". The rule is the sentence marketing will repeat most, so it is the one place the consent gate cannot be left to a later paragraph. Without it, copy built on the rule would say the engine remembers every visitor, which S2 marks as wrong.
2. **"Ranks your content" replaces "decides".** "Decides" says nothing a marketer can picture; "ranks your content" names what the engine returns (S2 §1: the brand's own content ids, in order, per slot) and keeps the site as the renderer.

Kept from the candidate: "not who they are" rather than "never" or "anonymous", because sign-in merges interests under a salted hash (S2 §3.1), so the precise claim is about what the engine keeps, not an absolute. "By the rules your team publishes" holds because every setting that changes an answer is published (S2 §1, §4), and the console itself calls the weights "Slot rules" (S2 §4.5). The visitor is "they", by the owner's gender-neutral ruling (section 9). The rule is 25 words against the candidate's 19; the six extra words carry the consent gate and the verb, and I judged both worth more than brevity.

The rule speaks to the customer ("your content", "your team"), as do the sentences to say, because those are the lines marketing repeats outward. The explanatory body speaks of "the brand" and "the brand's team", so the marketer reading it is never addressed as the one who publishes rules.

## 5. Where I was tempted to overstate, and did not

1. **Consent in the rule**, as in section 2: S2 §1 corrects "for each visitor".
2. **"Anonymous" and "never who they are."** Not used as absolutes: sign-in merges interests under a salted hash (S2 §3.1). The identity answer says so.
3. **"It remembers the visitor when they come back."** Not claimed. Recognizing a returning stranger is built and off until the customer turns it on (S2 §3.1).
4. **"Every term on the receipt."** Not claimed. The receipt shows what was served and why; its drivers list only the four strongest tag terms (S2 §3.14).
5. **"Everything that moves an answer is published."** Softened to "the settings that shape an answer", because S2 §1 corrects it: every setting is published, not every input, a few constants live in code, and worker settings need an engineer.
6. **"Counts from day one" and "a receipt for every decision".** Both kept, as the brief asks and S2 §10.1 rates Built, with the one exception flagged once below the step table: through the SDK, recording waits on durable recovery, which needs approved retention periods, an open decision (S2 §3.15, §10.2). The hosted demos record because they call the engine directly (S2 §3.15).
7. **"Exercised in the hosted demos" for every Built item.** Not claimed. Built means tests or a hosted demo (S2's label definition), and some Built items are not on show: the demos run holdouts at 0 percent (S2 §5.3).
8. **A speed figure.** None anywhere. The brief's never-say example "sub-50 millisecond decisions" is described ("any speed figure, such as a decision time in milliseconds") rather than printed, because the common brief forbids a latency figure "anywhere, for any reason".
9. **"Legacy."** The brief's own word for the second contrast is not used: S3 §1.4 finds no document calling another system legacy, and the common brief forbids judging another system. The contrast is with a mechanism.
10. **"Complementary to recommendations" or "works beside them".** Not claimed; S2 does not say it. The answer states only what S2 does: the engine ranks content, reorders a supplied product list, and picks no products from a catalog of its own (S2 §7.2, §3.3).
11. **"Opal creates audiences in ODP."** Not used; S2 §6.2 corrects the pricing guide.
12. **"Content tagging uses credits."** Stated as open (S2 §6.3), though the credits guide lists it under credits.
13. **"No AI ever touches the shopper."** Not claimed; typed search, which reads what a visitor types with the brand's own model, is named as separate and off by default (S2 §6.1, §6.3).
14. **Lift or revenue results.** Not claimed; the day report's inference is "unavailable" (S2 §3.16, §5.7).
15. **A native app kit.** Stated as absent (S2 §7.1: "never sell it as available").
16. **"Minutes to weeks".** The brief's wording, kept. S2 states the cap (90 days) and per-dimension memory (§3.4, §3.17) but no lower bound; the glossary's Memory entry notes that demos publish far shorter memories. The live value is open, and the piece says it is chosen with each customer.
17. **The regional prior** is offered as "can use", not as always on: it is a published setting (S2 §1, §3.6), and the demos' regional data is called synthetic and labeled (S2 §10.1).
18. **Holdouts "measuring" the engine on a customer site.** Dropped from the answers; holdouts appear only in the Built row, because recording on a customer site is the open exception.
19. **Erasure.** Not mentioned at all, rather than risk "one call and done" (S2 §8.8).
