## 2. The seeding model

Printed in the script's header and by `--dry-run`. Texas (490 sales) and New York (425) are lane L4's rows, unchanged byte for byte. The other 49:

```
x = clamp((state median household income − $81,604) / $25,000, −1, 1)
t = 0.5 + 0.3 · x                                      (0.2 … 0.8)
phones, per brand · ecosystem · price band row = TX(row) · (1 − t) + NY(row) · t
        400 a state, whole sales by largest remainder
home internet = 58 sales in the listed states, none elsewhere
```

- **Income** is the state's row in the shared `geo_census` table (Census ACS 2024 1-year, B19013), parsed from `migrations/seed/seed_011_geo.sql`, the file that loaded those rows; the read-back checks that each panel shows the same figure. Only DC reaches the cap (x = 1); Mississippi is lowest (x = −0.90, t = 0.23).
- **t = 0.5 is the national mix**, the two rehearsed regions pooled: iPhone 48.75 %, Samsung 33 %, Google 11 %, Motorola 7.5 %; premium 49 %, core 40 %, entry 11 %. Higher income moves a state toward New York's sales (iPhone, premium), lower toward Texas's (Android, core), never more than 0.3 from the middle. Every state's mix is a blend strictly between the two rehearsed regions, so none leans harder than either.
- **Home internet**, a demo list: fiber, the Fios footprint (NY NJ PA MA RI DE MD VA DC), and fixed wireless, the catalog's own 5G Home list (`promo-5g-home` `eligibleWhen.regions`: TX NY NJ PA MA IL FL GA NC CA AZ CO WA MI OH VA MN TN). 58 sales (the rehearsed regions' average of 90 and 25) in the 20 listed states besides NY and TX.
- **Never used or derived:** a protected class, household or family tables, line counts, intent, a ZIP. The census income shapes only this synthetic stand-in, and the on-screen label says so ("North Carolina: synthetic sales, national mix tilted by census income"). The engine reads no census figure at decision time (`scored.census` stays false); in production the lean is the carrier's own warehouse sales, not a model of them.

Examples (`--dry-run --rows`): Mississippi t 0.230, iPhone 171 of 400, Android leads the ecosystem, core leads the band; North Carolina t 0.408, iPhone 187, android 1 · ios 0.878, premium 1 · core 0.883, home internet 0.145; DC t 0.800, iPhone 222, ios 1 · android 0.802, premium 1 · core 0.607.

## 5. Findings, and what other owners must change

1. **A walkthrough rewrites its own state's lean, and one press with intent or lines is enough to take beat 1 off the ad (the most important finding).** Every event fans into the region the edge places the visitor in, on every dimension (`RealtimeSegmentEngine.ts:563`), and the prior blends every dimension of the region's shares (`service.ts:452-458`), each relative to its own leader. The seed writes only brand, ecosystem, price band and category, so intent, lines, perk and content type come entirely from live traffic, at full share from the first touch. Measured on the hosted worker: after the two suites (60 live events from this machine), a North Carolina stranger opened on 5G Home 1.417, the Duo 0.808, Pixel 11 0.666 and the launch offer 0.640, under the family hero; locally the same after one suite run. Before this lane the hazard was latent, because beat 1 asserted New York, which nobody's traffic reached. **Operation (done in the resume):** reseed the presenter's state with `--regions here` before every walkthrough and after any suite run (33 s hosted), and give the decision path's one-minute cache a minute. The panel now shows live events beside the seed. **Engine, for a ruling, not built:** let `learn.regional` name the dimensions it blends (here category, brand, ecosystem, price band), which is also what design §4 says (no cold-start prior on perk, lines "by rule", or content type; intent only from the campaign seed).
2. **`scripts/seed-vantage.mjs` (lane L1's) regenerates `01-arrival.json` and `02-another-region.json` on every reseed**, from `NEW_STRANGER.ctx` (`region: REGIONS.rehearsed[0].key`) and the beat-1 `engine` text (lines 676 and 704). The next morning reseed puts `ctx.region: US-NY` back into the beat-1 document. Nothing reads those two documents at run time (they are served and documented only), but the generator needs the same two changes as this lane's copy.
3. **Locally, `request.cf` is not empty**, as the brief expected: wrangler 4.105's Miniflare fills it from `node_modules/.mf/cf.json`, Cloudflare's own reading of this machine fetched on 2026-09-22 and kept 30 days (US-NC), and when it cannot fetch one it uses a fixed placeholder for Austin, Texas. So locally the panel names a region and says it comes from the local runtime, not an edge, rather than "geo real". The "no region on this request" branch (national grain, the route's reason, the no-region fixture) is built and covered by the suites' check, and runs only where `cf` carries no region.
4. **Beats 14a and 14b still assert New York and Montana**, the availability pair, labelled asserted, because the script's beat 14 says New York and nothing else in the script was to change. If the lead wants 14a in the presenter's own state when it is covered (North Carolina is on the 5G Home list), it is one line in `regions()` plus the script's beat-14 sentence.
5. **The local worker's state during this lane:** the ledger consumer was stalled at the start (receipts 202 for 16 s) and recovered on its own, and `LearnStats.priorsFor` threw "Configuration publication authority unavailable" repeatedly (L5 finding 3), restarting the runtime, so long requests in flight answered 503: 15 of the first 51 seed calls. The seed now asks again; nothing in L1's worker was restarted or touched.
6. **The hosted worker's `TREND_ROLLUP_TENANTS` is `coach`**, so the hourly cron does not roll `vantage` regions up into US and everyone; the seed route rolls up after each region, so the country level is current after a seed and drifts with live traffic afterwards. No effect while every state is seeded.
7. **Timing:** the full region seed takes about 4 minutes locally and 11 hosted (one durable-object call per synthetic sale, 12 to 25 s a region). The resume says so.
