# QVC Pursuit — Demo Design Brief (Lead Architect Synthesis)

**Status:** INTERNAL · 2026-08-18 · Synthesizes the three research archives in this folder (Business Intelligence · Competitive & Measurement · Site Recon & Demo Design). Those documents carry the evidence; this one carries the decisions.
**Timeline convention:** all effort figures are INTERNAL working estimates. External commitments are set separately, buffered, never from this document.

## 1. The demo in one paragraph

**"The Bright Hour"** — a fictional live-shopping retailer, built to read unmistakably as QVC's world (their design grammar, their offer constructs, their audience's accessibility needs) without their name — running on our real edge personalization engine at a new route in the demo platform. The centerpiece is the thing their own account team told us to show: **a short-lived offer entering the system, tagged with human approval, going eligible on its start time, ranked per visitor by two-speed affinity, expiring on its end time, and the next offer flowing in — no campaign built, no page rebuilt — with a warehouse-shaped decision row landing at the end.** Around that centerpiece: fourteen beats (recon Part C) covering the first-time anonymous visitor, several-offers-one-slot, governance against over-personalization, TV-to-web continuity, host affinity, and the glass box that explains — and sometimes *refuses* — every decision.

## 2. The five load-bearing truths (synthesis across all three dossiers)

1. **This is decisioning-under-expiry, not recommendations — and the incumbent structurally cannot do it.** QVC runs Adobe Target today (verified in their live tag container) on the legacy at.js stack. Adobe's own docs: models rebuild every 24h with per-day-per-experience learning floors; a new item takes 2–32h to become recommendable; the explainability report requires an activity live 15 days — *structurally impossible at QVC's offer cadence*; the flagship ML activity can't report through Adobe Analytics at all; no per-decision export exists without buying RTCDP. Their signature offer construct lives 24 hours; even their "24-hour" TSV is being stretched to 4.4 days (their own API shows it). **The line: "You don't have a recommendations problem. You have a decisioning-under-expiry problem."** And item-recs is Constructor's incumbent turf on both brands — offer/content decisioning is the open lane.
2. **The buyer is twelve days out of Chapter 11, and the crisis is first-session digital.** New owner-creditors, interim CEO, no guidance; new customers collapsed to 3% of shipped sales while 90% of new customers start digitally. Everything must be small, fast, provable inside one budget cycle — and **the demo's hero visitor is the anonymous first-timer**, not the loyal superfan every other vendor will show. Our engine's anonymous-first, no-training-period thesis is literally aimed at their most-bleeding number. ROI precedent is board-approved twice (Athens digital conversion optimization $39M→$87M; alby +1.5% conversion).
3. **They already built our POV and couldn't finish it.** `top-offers.json` (on a branch named `test-do-not-publish`): six category dimensions, each a timely offer + evergreen fallback — hand-curated. Five personalization feature flags built and switched off. The homepage hand-authored per calendar date against a machine that presents a new product every 6.4 minutes. The offer window already a first-class product field their page ignores. **Posture: we finish what you started; we made the page read the field you already populate.**
4. **No urgency theater — window state as language, the clock in the ops view only.** They deliberately hide countdowns, ship no scarcity counters, no social proof; their availability vocabulary is nine exact strings. Beat 11 personalizes urgency *only for visitors who demonstrated they respond to it, decaying by default* — the governance mechanism for a restraint they already practice. Same discipline as their 50+ audience's UI needs (16px Medium, 9.7:1 contrast, text resizer): **if the demo looks like a sleek DTC site, we didn't understand who shops there.**
5. **The proof is rows, not dashboards.** Their data science team passes test identifiers into their own reporting and computes their own uplift. Beat 7's decision-row schema (decision_id, gates, scores, experiment/variation IDs, holdback, config version) maps every KPI Kevin named to a column. **We hand them the rows; they compute the lift** — the exact posture Adobe's export paths can't match (documented in the competitive dossier §4, with our own honesty ledger §5 for the "is your content personalization in discovery?" question).

## 4. Decisions (yours)

1. **Brand name:** recommend **The Bright Hour** ("Today's Bright One" / "Bright Pay" / "The Bright Card"); four alternates in recon B1. ⚠️ "Today's Big Find" is off-limits (real QVC property).
2. **Route:** recommend **`/live`** (alternates: `/bright`, `/shopping`). Same static-assets mechanism as `/restaurant` — zero routing code.
3. **Imagery — RESOLVED 2026-08-18 (Simone challenged all-AI; hybrid adopted).** The recon's case against QVC's own imagery stands (defeats the built-not-mocked premise; usage rights; breaks the sparse-metadata tagging story). But all-AI fails predictably in **electronics** (invented devices) and **jewelry** (impossible macro detail), and beauty packaging carries the AI-text trap. **The hybrid:** (a) real photography for electronics + jewelry — permissive stock (Unsplash/Pexels class licenses), unbranded selections, **background-normalized to the white packshot standard** (deterministic edit, not generation); (b) AI generation only in its proven categories — kitchen, home, garden, food, fashion flat-lays (no bodies), lifestyle heroes, host portraits (AI is *safer* than free stock for people: stock carries no model releases); beauty designed as minimalist unlabeled vessels to dodge label text; (c) **one normalization pipeline over everything** (white background, margin, shadow) so the grid reads as one catalog regardless of source; (d) **the no-surprises protocol**: 3–4 candidates per item at 2x, defect checklist (hands, text, impossible geometry), ~40% rejection budget, human-eye curation on every image — AI is reliable per curated batch, never per image; (e) optional fallback: a small licensed-stock spend (~10–15 hero images) if free stock can't cover a gap. Open datasets (e.g. Amazon ABO packshots) are non-commercially licensed — unusable for a sales demo without legal review; do not use.
4. **The live-data exhibit (optional, powerful):** QVC's on-air grid and product API are open — a presenter-only exhibit could feed *their real on-air feed of that day* through the same lifecycle engine ("and this is your actual grid, today"). Taste/ToS judgment; the demo stands complete without it.
5. **FX project** for the experimentation beats (same identifier handoff as the PH list).
6. **Build approval** — no build starts until you approve this document set (house rule).
