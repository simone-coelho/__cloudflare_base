## 2. The Meridian content lane — what it proves, and its limits

`src/demos/meridian/content.ts`, `content.catalog.json`, and `contentCompose.ts` are new since the 07-24 ledger (git log: "Meridian: the catalogue is families x colourways..." and related commits, most recently 2026-08-28/29) and they are a real, useful validation of the Tapestry thesis — worth being precise about what they do and do not close:

**What is genuinely proven:**
- The scoring formula generalizes to content without modification: `contentCompose.ts:85-96` sums `affinity[dim][value] × slot.weights[dim]` over a piece's tags — structurally identical to the product-ranking pattern and to Doc 18 §5's formula.
- Dual-ID addressing works end to end: every piece carries both `systemId` (ours) and `customerContentId` (e.g. `"CMP-1001"`, deliberately styled like the doc's own `CMP1234` examples — `content.catalog.json:4`), and the wire payload echoes `customerContentId` back (`contentCompose.ts:48,75,105`; shown live in `public/meridian/meridian.js:283-289`, `contentWire()`).
- Pin-at-any-position and completion-preference (a demonstrated, tested "content changes jobs when she commits" behavior, `meridian.js:232-235`) are real, working, novel pieces of the governance model that go beyond what the 07-24 ledger had evidence for.
- A loud, named validation guard (`content.ts:93-121`) catches a mistagged content piece at load time rather than letting it fail silently at serving time — a good pattern the productized build should keep.

**What is not proven, and should not be read as "R1/R3/R5 are basically done":**
- It is a **client-side** computation over a **full catalog dump** (`routes.ts:24-59` ships everything at page load), not a server-side decision service. The productized contract (Doc 18 D-4) is "decisions by ID over the socket" — the engine decides, the customer paints. Meridian's pattern is closer to "the browser decides, using logic and data we shipped it," which is architecturally the opposite of what's being sold, and isn't just a wiring gap — shipping ranking logic and a full content/product catalog into a customer's page bundle is a different (and generally worse, for a real customer) posture than a decision-by-ID push.
- **Zero content telemetry** — confirmed by direct grep, not inferred: there is no `content_impression`/`content_click`/`content_dwell` anywhere, including in Meridian. The content vector that `composeContent` scores against is entirely the *product*-side reflex vector (tags shared, per Doc 18's design) — nothing about a shopper's response to content itself feeds back in.
- Content decisions are **never persisted**. `src/demos/meridian/receipts.ts`'s `capture()`/`exportRows()` pipeline is real and does write product/layout decisions to D1 (`migrations/0007_meridian_decisions.sql`), but `public/meridian/meridian.js:2523-2532` (`captureDecisions`) only ever posts product decisions (`ds`, keyed on `itemId`/`blockId`) to `POST /meridian/api/decisions`; the content payload is rendered read-only into the on-page "Receipts" panel (`meridian.js:2550-2554`, `contentWire()`) and never leaves the browser. So the "full data egress" claim in §0 is not even fully true *for the demo*, let alone for a customer.
- Slot weights are hardcoded JS (`contentSlots()`, `meridian.js:228-236`), not the KV-versioned, tunable object T2/M6 promise.
- The catalog schema gap noted in §1.3 (no render URL, no lifecycle windows) means the content catalog has not been exercised against anything resembling a real customer feed.

**Bottom line for whoever picks this up next:** treat the Meridian content lane as a validated algorithm design and a UI/interaction reference, not as a head start on CW2/CW3/CW4/CW5's actual deliverables. The productized build still needs to be written server-side, from a real content catalog store, wired to real telemetry, exposed through auth, and persisted — none of which the demo needed to do, because it's a demo.
