# NYT Pursuit — Strategy Addendum

## The thesis, sharpened
**Don't out-model them — out-orchestrate them.** Every published NYT ML system is single-surface (the paywall meter, the recommender bandits, homepage curation). Their attribution model *measures* cross-property paths; **nothing acts on them.** We are not competing with their models — we operate *between* them. Cross-property bundle orchestration (Games ↔ Cooking ↔ News ↔ Athletic journeys) is the seam.

## The lead use case flips
**Cross-property bundle orchestration is the lead.** The paywall/regwall is the *destination* of that journey — not the thing we claim to improve. Their bundle economics justify it on their own numbers: bundle ARPU $12.67 vs single-product $3.47 (3.6×), and 2026 exec comp quietly moved from digital-subscription revenue to **total** revenue (ads and licensing matter again).

## The wall: a probe question, never a claim
Softening required: Rohit Supekar's own bio covers "access **and messaging** systems" — assume partial internal coverage of offer/messaging at the wall. In the room it is a QUESTION: *"Does anything today carry what she did in Games into what the wall says?"* If no — the gap surfaced itself, live. If yes — pivot to the bundle journey, nothing lost. **Never assert it cold.**

## The adoption lesson = our design requirement
Newsroom adoption of their homepage algorithms was **blocked until editor observability existed** (browser extension, change alerts, dashboards). Consequence: **the Glass Box, story ledger, and decision records are the acceptance criteria at NYT, not garnish.** Our existing presenter surface is closer to their internal acceptance bar than any vendor UI.

Two beats to steal from their own vocabulary:
- **Exposure minimums** — "all stories receive a minimum number of impressions before the algorithm takes over." That is our discovery quota in their words: a credential, not a differentiator. Say it as parity, not novelty.
- **The reader's own dial** — their public editor: *"If readers feel they're getting too much of a particular subject, let them scale it back."* A reader-facing personalization control is a beat almost nobody demos, and it is on-brand for NYT specifically.

## The door, and the clock
- **Josh Laurito — SVP Data since March 2026** (ex-Squarespace), explicit mandate: "a unified data strategy… using machine learning to further our goals." A new executive with a cross-cutting mandate is the way in — and cross-surface orchestration is precisely that mandate's shape.
- The clock: 15M by end-2027 with zero slack (280K delivered vs ~275K/qtr needed).

## Negative finding worth stating
**No third-party experimentation, CDP, or ESP vendor surfaces anywhere in NYT's own writing.** The absence is the finding. Piano is the category frame a publisher CFO recognizes — but everything Piano sells, NYT built.
