**How to draw over the art.** Use white type everywhere (`ink: "light"` on all 44). Each file's `zones` gives three boxes as frame fractions, `[left, top, right, bottom]`:
- `text`: where the headline, sub and button go. Slides and banners: the left 56 %. Tiles: bottom left, `[0.05, 0.58, 0.5, 0.94]`, as lg.com's bento sets its headlines. Tall: the bottom 30 %.
- `product`: the placed product's own box. Never draw over it.
- `badge`: a corner the product leaves clear, or `null` for banners.

No product overlaps its file's text zone. White type's contrast is at least 6:1 at the 90th percentile of the text zone's luminance on every file (lowest `deal_laundry_pair/tall`, 6.0:1). Most files are 10:1 to 19:1.

## How it was made

- **Looked first.** lg.com/us and lg.com/us/tvs at 1440. The hero slides are 1600×600 art: a TV in a dusk stadium with ember-orange light, a warm laundry room, appliances on a red-glow ground. LG's badges sit in the image's upper right and the product fills the right of the frame. The "Discover LG Deals" bento uses image tiles with white headlines bottom left over a darkened foot and a red arrow. **One observation for M1b:** today's homepage and TVs hero sets the headline in dark type on a white curved panel over the left of the slide, rather than white type on the art. The art works either way, because the product sits in the right two fifths and the left is calm.
- **The scenes** came from the model `generate-vantage-art.mjs` uses (`gemini-3.1-flash-image`, 2K) at 21:9, 16:9 and 4:5, under one locked recipe: rich, dark, cinematic, one soft colour glow, eye-level one-point perspective. The recipe forbids text, letters, logos, people, screens and appliances, and leaves the right of the frame empty and calm on the left for the headline.
- **Eleven sets:**
  - living rooms: amber stadium-light glow, navy with blue-violet cove light, espresso panelling with a long oak console
  - studios: deep burgundy `#4a0a1e` with a magenta glow, black with red/green/blue glows, charcoal with a blue glow, near-black with an emerald glow
  - laundry rooms: sage-green, burgundy panelled
  - kitchens: night kitchen with a lit stone wall, walnut kitchen with a pendant
- **The loop** was the Vantage one:
  1. Roll one candidate per job plus a spare (72), then 4 re-rolls for two tall scenes whose floors had a hard band. The framing wording was revised for those, and each candidate's exact prompt is kept beside it.
  2. Review contact sheets of every candidate.
  3. Pick per job and fit the layout (centre, base line, box) to the console top or floor line of the picked frame.
  4. Compose, check at 1:1, fix, recompose.
  5. Bind each verdict to the sha256.
- **The product** is the piece's lead (`display.imageSku`), from D2's WebP in `products/`, with its file and sha256 recorded per row. It sits in the right two fifths (upper middle for tall). Wall-hung TVs get a soft wall shadow. Anything standing gets a contact shadow, plus a fading reflection on glossy studio floors. Treatments, all recorded per row:
  - LG's composited labels on the ground erased. These are whole detached parts inside a box, never the product: the S90TY "perfect match" card, the Sound Suite wordmarks, and the "America's Most Reliable" shield on the package and the laundry set.
  - The white glow LG's ground left around the dark soundbar and Sound Suite faded out.
  - Ragged white ground under white appliances' bases cleared, with the dark feet kept.
  - Specks of under 60 px removed.
  - The edge pulled in by one pixel.
  - The C6's stand cropped for the wall-mounting deal.
- **Substitution:** LG's image of LDNTH753S, the lead for `deal_fridge_dishwasher` and `tile_dishwashers`, carries a Consumer Reports badge across its door, which only editing the product could remove. LDNPM654S, a stainless dishwasher from the same piece's featured list, is placed instead. The row's `substituteFor` and `why` say so.
- **Room banners** use each room's broadest product: the C6 for TVs, the washer and dryer set for laundry, the four-piece package for the kitchen.
- **Text:** none is baked in. The only marks are LG's own on its products: the screens' "LG OLED evo AI" and "LG Micro RGB evo AI" artwork and the StanbyME's screen, as LG publishes them (D2's ruling).

## Verdicts

All 44 are `accept`, checked 2026-10-02 on contact sheets and at 1:1. The note lives in `art.json` `gate` and the manifest row's `gate`, with the rejected candidates and why:
- a hard band across a tall floor (studio-rgb a and b, studio-charcoal a to c, laundry-evening a, kitchen-wide b and c)
- a plinth that reads as an object
- a green glow that reads as a lit screen
- angled consoles under straight-on TVs
- cabinetry filling the product side

Fixed during review:
- a white halo around the soundbar (dark matte added)
- reflections that mirrored the top of the screen: sharp extracts before it flips, so the flip now runs in its own pipeline
- products overlapping the tile text zone (layouts moved)
- a pale floor under a tall headline (scrim raised)
- white specks and a ragged base under appliances (despeck and base trim)

Recomposing is byte-identical: the shas held across runs. A compose with no candidates keeps every reviewed file and row unchanged, which I verified.

## Deviations, stated once

- **AVIF replaces the kit's transparent PNG as the third encoding.** Opaque art has no transparency to carry. A PNG of these 44 files measured 4.45 MB at the kit's 256-colour setting and 2.86 MB at D2's 48-colour setting; either would take the payload past 25 MiB, which had 3.57 MiB of headroom. The manifest rows keep the kit's shape otherwise: `files`, `bytes` per encoding, `sha256` of the WebP, `size`, `generated`, `gate`.
- **Large placements were first drawn by upscaling D2's 420-pixel cutouts up to 1.5×.** This is resolved: since D2's 840 cutouts landed, every slide, banner and tile that would upscale draws the 840 file instead (above). The 19 files still on the 420 cutouts draw it at 0.68–0.93×.
- **The JPEG fallback is at q70.** That keeps the whole directory, manifest included, under 25 MiB. WebP is at q80 and AVIF at q56.

## Regenerate

Run under WSL, because `node_modules` holds the Linux sharp:

```bash
node scripts/generate-oh-art.mjs --roll --work <dir>                     # model calls; GEMINI_API_KEY by name from .dev.vars
node scripts/generate-oh-art.mjs --sheet <png> --work <dir>              # every candidate
node scripts/generate-oh-art.mjs --preview <dir> --picked --work <dir>   # each job as it would ship
node scripts/generate-oh-art.mjs --compose --review <dir> --work <dir>   # write art/, the index, the manifest's art section; contact sheets
```

A re-roll is a new review: a changed file reads `pending` until its verdict is re-bound in `art.json` `gate`. `--gradient` composes on the scenes' gradients with no model, as the fallback for a checkout without the credential.
