## Findings

### 1. DEFECT: a fence refusal that is not a 401 is remembered as the tenant's datafile failure, across shoppers

**Where.** `src/services/OptimizelyService.ts`:
- `:111` — `refusal` recognises only `HTTPException` 401;
- `:279` — the `fetchDatafile` catch;
- `:319` — the `createFreshClient` catch;
- `:269` and `:313` — the remembered failure is re-thrown to later callers.

**Why it happens.**
1. `ownerFetch` → `ownerEffect` rechecks the owner before and after the fetch (`src/identity/sessionAuthority.ts:279`, `:282`).
2. That recheck runs `requireRetention` on every pinned retention guard (`sessionAuthority.ts:320`).
3. `requireRetention` throws `RetentionUnavailable` (`src/retention.ts:96-99`), which extends `Error`, not `HTTPException` (`retention.ts:38`).
4. The memory is keyed by the backing binding and the tenant's connector (`:144-150`), not by the shopper. So one shopper's lapsed profile-retention stamp is stored as the whole tenant's datafile failure.
5. For 60 s, every other shopper's decision seam and feature-variable probes in that isolate degrade at once, and nothing is fetched.

**Evidence.** My probe `scratchpad/r6/probe-retention.test.ts` runs on the lane's code with the real `runOwnerOperation`, `retentionBirth` and `pinRetention`:
- Shopper A's pinned stamp lapses while the fetch is in flight, and A's operation rejects with `RetentionUnavailable`.
- 59 s later, shopper B (outside any owner operation, nothing lapsed) gets the same error instance, with 1 fetch in total.
- Control: after `forgetRememberedDatafiles()`, B fetches (2 fetches) and gets the datafile's own 503.
- Result: 1 passed.

**Why the test missed it.** `OptimizelyService.datafileMemory.test.ts` case (d) simulates the refusal as a global `fetch` that throws `SessionAccessError`, so it never reaches the fence's other refusal.

**How likely.** It needs a retention stamp that lapses, or a retention policy revision, during a datafile fetch. That is rare. The effect is up to 60 s of fallback decisions for every shopper of an FX tenant in the isolate.

**What would close it.** Remember only failures of the fetch, the response or the datafile validation. Never remember anything the owner fence raises.

### 4. Cache keys and invalidation: correct, and the tenant is in every key

**Publication** (`src/config/publication.ts:315-335`):
- **Reuse rule.** It reuses an entry only when the scope (the tenant, or its owner scope) and the committed revision and digest all match (`:323`).
- **Why ignoring the storage handle is safe.** The set digest covers the scope and every member's document digest, and every document was digest-checked when first read (`:363`). The cache is therefore content-addressed: no tenant can be served another tenant's set or documents.
- **Head validated every time.** Each owner operation gets a fresh fenced handle (`ownerEnvironment`, `sessionAuthority.ts:568-611`), so the resident lookup at `:318` misses and `requiredHead` runs at `:319`. The head is read and validated on every action in the session host.
- **Immutable.** The set and documents are deep-frozen (`freeze`, `:293`; `publicRevision`).
- **Republish honoured.** A new committed identity misses `known` and reads its own set. Test (d) shows it on the very next action.
- **Unchanged.** The administrative read (`servingCache=false`) never uses `known`.

**Datafile:**
- **Key.** Binding, then `t:<tenant>:connector:fx:<digest>|<url>` for customer tenants (`:144-150`, `cacheKey`). The legacy demo uses `optimizely-datafile-<sdkKey>|<url>`, which has no tenant by design but is the same key the pre-existing KV copy uses, and the same URL.
- **Key changes.** Rotating the key changes the digest and the URL, so a stale entry cannot be reused.
- **A key revoked upstream.** A remembered client serves for at most 10 s.
- **Failure window.** A failure is masked for exactly < 60 s from the failed fetch. The `at` is never refreshed without a new fetch, and test (b) checks the boundary.
- **A 401 is never remembered.** Any other fence refusal is (finding 1).

**Audiences:**
- **Key.** Binding, then tenant (`AudienceStore.ts:68-78`).
- **Writes.** Every write path in the product goes through the store, so `written()` covers it (`audienceGenerator.ts:189/203/217`, `RealtimeSegmentEngine.ts:330`, `operator.ts:41/445/497`).
- **Races.** A list that a write overtook is not kept (writes counter).

**Overrides:**
- **Key.** Binding, then `tenant\nshopper` (`FeatureVariableManager.ts:69-82`).
- **Expiry.** Each override's own expiry is still checked on read.

### 6. Staleness across isolates and erasure

**Audiences.** Something written in another isolate is seen up to 10 s later, on top of KV's own propagation. The only such writers are the operator and Opal routes running in the worker while the shopper's object decides. OH writes no audiences: `ensureAudiencesSeeded` returns for non-default tenants (`RealtimeSegmentEngine.ts:328`). I found no current contract that this breaks. The legacy Coach spec says "immediately qualifiable" (`docs/architecture/legacy/05-demo-build-spec-2026-06.md:356`), meaning with no redeploy.

**Overrides.** Nothing in the product writes an override: `setUserOverride` and `removeUserOverride` are reached only from `RealtimeSegmentEngine.setFeatureVariableOverride`/`remove…`, which have no non-test caller. So every remembered list is empty.

**Erasure leaves a theoretical gap.** Erasure deletes `override:<id>:` keys directly in KV (`src/identity/erase.ts:300`) and does not forget the in-isolate list. If an override writer is ever mounted, erased override records could be applied for up to 10 s in an isolate that listed them. Low, and theoretical today.

### 11. Step 4: the lane's reading of W05.02 is right

**Reproduced in memory** (`scratchpad/r6/early.vitest.config.mjs` applies the lane's `scratchpad/e4/early-answer.patch` to `ShopperReflex.ts` as it loads):

| early answer | drain the setup action | `realtime.sdkContract.test.ts:5673` (W05.02) |
|---|---|---|
| off | no | passes |
| on | no | fails: `expected [ 'outcome', 'learning' ] to deeply equal []` |
| on | yes (in memory, after the setup action at `:5686`) | passes |
| off | yes | passes |

Across the whole file, 44 fail with the early answer and 44 without. W05.02 is the only one added; W15 swapped, as the flake does. So the effects the test sees belong to the first, consenting setup action at `:5686`, not to the refused action it measures. The test assumes that an answered action's effects have finished by the time it answers.

**Whether moving the fan-out behind the answer is safe in principle.** For this shopper's order, yes. `ShopperReflex.serialize` (`ShopperReflex.ts:3267-3276`) chains the whole owner operation, and `runOwnerOperation` drains every retained task before it settles (`sessionAuthority.ts:472-485`). The lane's patch chains the operation, not the answer, so:
- the next operation for this shopper still starts only after the previous one's region ingest, outcome, ledger enqueue, capture and broadcast have settled;
- every effect still rechecks grant, consent, retention and recovery deadlines at its binding;
- frames, region counts, outcomes and ledger messages keep their per-shopper order;
- the outcome's attribution and ledger record id are fixed in `emitOutcome` before delivery (the lane's E4 §7; I did not re-verify this one).

What changes is what a 200 from `POST /realtime/action` means:
- **Today.** A 200 means the retained effects were admitted. A rejection, or a consent, retention or recovery deadline that lapses while they run, turns the answer into an error, because `runOwnerOperation` rethrows the first rejection and rechecks the owner at the end. W06.12 at `:793-828` encodes this for the broadcast.
- **After the move.** A 200 no longer implies either, and a failure is only logged. A client that retries on an error would no longer retry those cases.
- **Visibility.** Readers outside this shopper's chain (the RegionTrend and DecisionRing objects, the ledger and day report, receipt reads, other shoppers) could observe an action's effects after its answer rather than before. Reads through the same shopper object, such as the profile read the page sends next, still wait on the chain.

The ruling is therefore about whether that acknowledgement contract may change. The test edit follows from that ruling.
