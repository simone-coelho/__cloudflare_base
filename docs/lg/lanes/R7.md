## Findings

### 1. DEFECT: the ledger copy keeps an erased visitor's rows while the open hour's working fold is refused

**Where.** All in `src/learn/hourly.ts`:
- `:2292` — `keepLedgerCopy` is called only after `foldWorkingHour` returns (`:2289`), inside the `try`.
- `:2213–2229` — `keepLedgerCopy` is the only code that drops a segment naming a tombstoned visitor (`names`, `:2216`).
- `:2304–2307` — the `catch` records the refusal and moves on.
- `:2318–2325` — the end-of-run removal keeps every hour still open.

**Why it happens.**
- **Any refusal of the working fold skips the pruning.**
  - a `ReportRowConflict`;
  - the hour past `rawBytes`, because `loadHourRecords` charges copied and read objects to one 16 MiB budget;
  - `OpenHourDeferred`;
  - a retention refusal.
- **Report builds never prune.**
- **No other process removes the segments.** They live under `aggregates/<tenant>/open/`, and the erasure rewrite and the retention sweep never visit that prefix (`src/ledger/erasure.ts:714`: "Only the established raw record partition, never claims/config/aggregates").
- **The engine's existing derived store does better.** The rings are cleaned by `cleanupHourlyState` at the start of every catch-up (`hourly.ts:1929`), whether or not a fold then succeeds.

**Evidence.** My probe `scratchpad/r7/probes/open-hour.test.ts`, case "the copy keeps an erased visitor's rows while the open hour's working fold is refused":
- a conflicting row is written into hour 10, and visitor `v5` is tombstoned at 10:22;
- the job runs at 10:25, 10:30, … 10:55 are all refused with "conflicting rows under one logical id";
- the segment holding `v5`'s rows is present after every one of those seven runs;
- the 11:10 run, the first after the hour's grace, removes it.

On the success path the guarantee holds: in the tick-by-tick case (finding 6), after the erasure of `v7` no segment held `"visitor_id":"v7"` at any run.

**How far it reaches.**
- **Nothing beyond the ledger's own copy.** Today's ledger keeps the same rows until the next day's rewrite (`erasure.ts:480`, `nextOf(t) < today`). The copy goes at most about 70 minutes after the erasure.
- **OH erases no one.** Its suites' `forgetTab` is browser-side.
- **Two refusals do occur.** A conflict is rare. The raw-budget refusal is reachable on rehearsal traffic: the rig's hour 11 held 16,414,501 of 16,777,216 bytes, and my `mem/over.mjs` refuses an hour at 16,921,405.

**What would close it.** Run the segment pruning for tombstoned visitors on every job run, before the fold or regardless of it, plus a test with a refused fold.

### 6. The working aggregate is the final fold, step for step

- **The fold.**
  - `foldWorkingHour` (`:2161`) is `buildHour`'s non-pending path, minus the transaction.
  - The shards are cleaned in memory as `cleanupHourlyState` would clean them (`:2145–2152`).
  - The row filter, phase one, membership and brand close are the shared helpers.
  - On a first fold `refold` is false.
- **Tick by tick** (probe 2).
  - At every job run, each hour the run folded was compared, without `generation` and `builtAt`, with `buildHour` on a copy of the store at the same moment. The grace hour was folded first, as the job does.
  - The store held three busy hours, a tombstone at 10:40 and an object rewritten in place at 11:15.
  - Ticks at :05 gave 38 of 38 equal. Ticks at :02 gave 39 of 39, including 11:02 (hours 10 and 11) and 13:02 (hours 12 and 13).
  - A worked store and a quiet store produce the same final aggregates and all 64 shards byte for byte.
- **A build while the job runs.**
  - A segment the job deletes under a build reads `null`, and its objects are read from the ledger (`:2110`).
  - A segment the job adds after the build's listing is simply not used.
  - The working aggregate is last-writer-wins between job and build, and each write is a whole fold.
- **A torn shard read is possible (informational).**
  - `committedShards` checks shard 0's pending marker once, first, and never reads it again (`:2145–2152`).
  - A build that overlaps the catch-up's final-fold writes, at about HH:05, can fold the open hour on a mix of pre- and post-fold shards.
  - This is transient. The job reads the shards after the catch-up in the same task, and its next change rewrites the working aggregate.
- **No double count.** Each listed key is served once, from the copy or from storage (`:1292–1294`). Redelivered rows across objects or segments go through the same row identity as the final fold.
- **The job skipped.**
  - A build refreshes only hours the job started (`:2267`).
  - With no job, a day reads raw exactly as before (lane test 12).
  - If the job stops mid-hour, the build reads the remainder from the ledger, inside the same caps.
  - Working objects left behind are removed by the next job run, and stay if the job never runs again.
- **The hour closing.** The grace hour keeps its working aggregate until its fold is due. At HH:05 the catch-up runs before the open-hour fold in the same task (`index.ts:295–301`).
- **Sequencing.** The open-hour fold runs only if `catchUp` returns. `catchUp` throws at its first line when `cleanupHourlyState`'s plan exceeds the case-2 bound (finding 11), and then the open-hour fold does not run either.

### 11. The third cause, the fold stall: the lane's reading is right

**Where `aggregateBytes` refuses** (`hourly.ts`):
- `:1810` — the staged shard bodies;
- `:1827` — the prepared shard-0 body, which holds every staged body JSON-escaped inside the pending plan, plus the aggregate;
- `:1829–1830` — that body plus max(old, new) of every other shard, "every possible partially written mixture".

**Why about 7.5 MB.** That is roughly two copies of the 48-hour ring state against one 16 MiB budget. This agrees with the rig's 22,205,992 against rings of 6,652,548.

**What follows in `catchUp`.** It stops after two other failures (`:1994–1995`), so two refused hours at the front of the lookback hold every newer hour back.

**The lane neither fixes nor worsens it.**
- The working fold writes no shard. Probe 2 shows identical shards with and without it.
- What it adds is a refold of the open hour to each run of an invocation that, in case 2, already re-reads every refused hour's raw.

**What the ruling turns on.** The ruling turns on what the final fold's recovery record has to carry. Today the version-2 plan is "self-contained recovery, never rebuilt against changed raw rows" (`:1328`). It holds every staged shard body and the aggregate, and its bound adds both versions of every other shard, so the 48-hour rings can be at most about 7.5 MB. The remediation track can choose among four things:

- **(a)** a recovery format that does not carry whole or unchanged shards, such as staging keys or digests. That is W30.04's transaction.
- **(b)** a smaller ring for a demo tenant: a shorter horizon or slimmer entries. That is W22's horizon and the A1.01 attribution contract the day report states.
- **(c)** a transaction budget of its own instead of the report's `aggregateBytes`. Moving a refusal budget is a W22 decision, not a lane's.
- **(d)** separately, whether an hour past its own raw budget is truncated as it is at the object cap.

**Two facts make it more pressing than E6.md §6 says.**
- `cleanupHourlyState`'s plan carries the same two-copies bound (`:1570–1572`) and runs first in every catch-up (`:1929`). So on busy rings a single erasure or retention expiry stops the whole catch-up, not only the busy hours.
- With this lane, that also stops the open-hour fold (finding 6).

### S1. Defect 1 (erasure): closed

**The mechanism** (`hourly.ts:2413–2431` `pruneCopy`; `:2562` the call):
- On every job run, first, the job deletes:
  - every segment whose state index names a visitor with a pending erasure;
  - every segment the state does not index.
- This happens before the basis check, before the budget check and whatever the fold then does.
- The erased set comes from the names in one tombstone listing (`:2349–2357`, `erasedVisitors`). No tombstone and no segment is read.
- Matching is by `visitor_id`. That is the field the ledger's own rewrite matches (`erasure.ts:443–453`).
- `index.ts:308–309` now catches the catch-up's failure and goes on to the open hour.

### S5. What else is in the delta

1. **`index.ts`'s counting wrapper drops `get`'s options (latent; one line).**
   - **Where.** `src/index.ts:299–300`: `get: (key) => { calls++; return storage.get(key); }`.
   - **What it breaks.** The catch-up's `readWindowSummary` asks for `{ range: { offset: 0, length: 65,536 } }` (`report.ts:539`). Through the wrapper it gets the whole saved report.
   - **Where it refuses.** `summaryPrefix` charges every received chunk to `summaryBytes` (`report.ts`, `add`). A first chunk larger than 64 KiB refuses.
   - **Evidence** (`probes/wrapper.test.ts`, an R2 fake that honours `range`):
     - with a 1,289,106-byte saved report, chunks of 16,384 and 65,536 bytes read fine through the wrapper;
     - chunks of 131,072 bytes refuse with `summaryBytes` (observed 131,072);
     - the same read without the wrapper is fine at every size.
   - **What happens if it refuses.** The catch-up records "day report publication failed" for that date and does not republish it in runs that build nothing. Republishing a saved day that names a closed hour open (S2) depends on that path.
   - **What I found on real workerd.** The lane's rig ran this path through the same wrapper. In `scratchpad/e6/rig-3.log` there are 17 catch-up publication lines, all with `failed 0`, and no failure line in runs that built nothing. So local R2 streams arrive in chunks of 64 KiB or less. I cannot say what hosted R2 delivers.
   - **The fix.** Pass the options through: `get: (key, options) => storage.get(key, options)`. That restores 272ede8's read exactly.
2. **`hours.open` validation is strict.** A malformed entry would refuse the day. Every producer is the engine, and all its codes, messages and figures pass `openRefusal` (`:1101–1110`).
3. **The note's corrections are honest.** E6.md now corrects my first look's two overstatements (the key order, the erasure sentence) and records the second build and its rig. I did not re-run the rig. The lane's figures there sit beside the in-process counts above, which agree.
