# Lane E6 — the day report answers on a busy day

Engine lane, briefed by the lead on 2026-10-03 after the hosted verification of OH's version `974f1c2f`
(commit `272ede8`). The hosted suites passed everything that is the page's (request level 223 of 226, DOM 495 of
498) and failed six checks on one engine answer: `POST /oh/api/report {"date":"2026-10-03","slot":"pdp-modules",
"build":true}` answered **413 "report too large for the window"**, so beat 16 ("Best Buy counts", the day report
on screen with its orders and attach) showed the refusal instead of the report. The same refusal ended three
lanes' rig days this session once their states had carried a day of suite runs (S2, M1d, M3b each prepared a
fresh state to get past it), and the lead's own hosted day report on 2026-10-02 answered 413 after a heavy
rehearsal day. A presenter who rehearses in the morning and presents in the afternoon of the same UTC day can
meet it.

## What is known

- The kit's report route (`src/demos/kit/actionRoutes.ts` about line 349–374) asks the engine for one whole UTC
  day for the tenant and passes a 413 through as `report_too_large` with the engine's `budget`, `limit` and
  `observed` (`src/demos/kit/helpers.ts` `reportTooLarge`). The engine has no slot or hour filter: "a busy day
  can outgrow the budget whatever the page asks for".
- The budgets are `REPORT_LIMITS` in `src/learn/report.ts` line 27: records 50,000; policies 6; objects 800;
  objectBytes, rawBytes, aggregateBytes and windowBytes 16 MiB each; work 1,000,000; cells 16,384; outputBytes
  and savedBytes 8 MiB; requestBytes 64 KiB; aggregateObjects 24; summaryBytes 64 KiB. They are "application
  refusal budgets, not measured Worker capacity or a data-retention policy" (remediation W22, F16 §5(j) and
  neighbours; `ReportRowConflict` refuses a day with two different rows under one id).
- The hourly fold (`src/learn/hourly.ts`) folds a closed hour's ledger objects into one aggregate object some
  time after the hour ends; the day report reads the folded hours' aggregates plus the unfolded hours' raw
  objects. The OH worker's crons are `*/5 * * * *`, `0 * * * *` and `0 3 * * *` (`wrangler.oh.toml` line 159).
  S2 observed that "folding the closed hours did not help, because the open hour holds the runs": one hour of
  suite traffic (a DOM run makes about 500 checks with hundreds of events and receipts) is enough.
- Which budget tripped hosted is not in the run log (the page's line was cut); the lane establishes it.

## What the lane does

1. **Reproduce and name it.** On your rig, drive one hour of traffic equal to two hosted suite runs (run the
   two OH suites twice against the rig inside one UTC hour, or replay their events faster through the kit's
   seed library), then ask the kit's report route for `pdp-modules` with `build: true`. Record the budget, the
   limit and the observed figure, and the raw objects' count and bytes behind them. Put the figures in the note.
2. **Bound the build without raising a budget.** The refusal budgets exist for a reason and stay; what changes
   is how much raw the day report has to read. The intended shape, for the lane to confirm or better: the
   open hour is folded incrementally by the five-minute cron into the hour's working aggregate (closed
   five-minute segments folded, the current segment raw), so the day report reads at most one aggregate per
   hour plus a few minutes of raw, and the hour's final fold at the top of the next hour produces the same
   aggregate object it produces today (byte-equal for the same records, so the W22 contracts on the fold hold:
   erased visitors leave at the next fold, provenance-less records write the bytes they always did, row
   conflicts still refuse, `foldedHours` still names the hours). If a different design bounds it better (a
   report built per slot from the rings; a streamed raw read within `work`), argue it in the note first and
   build it only if it keeps every contract above.
3. **Never make a check pass by loosening.** No budget in `REPORT_LIMITS` rises; the W22 tests under
   `src/units/W22/**` and `src/learn/*.test.ts` stay as they are and stay green; add tests for the incremental
   fold (a segment folded, the hour's final aggregate byte-equal to today's for the same records, an erased
   visitor gone from the working aggregate at the next segment fold, a day report that reads one aggregate per
   closed hour plus the raw tail).
4. **Prove it.** The same hour of traffic as step 1, with the five-minute cron run on the rig as
   `docs/oh/RESUME-oh.md` shows (`curl "http://localhost:<port>/cdn-cgi/handler/scheduled?cron=*/5+*+*+*+*"`),
   then the report route answers 200 for every slot in the kit's `reportSlots`, and the OH DOM suite's beat 16
   passes on that state (`node scripts/rehearse-oh-dom.mjs --base http://localhost:<port>`). Then three times
   that hour of traffic, the same. Report the raw bytes the report read before and after.
5. **Gate.** The touched test files, `src/learn/**/*.test.ts`, `src/units/W22/**`, `src/routes/oh.test.ts`,
   `src/demos/kit/*.test.ts`; `npm run check:generated`; the app typecheck `npx tsc --noEmit --incremental false
   --composite false`. The baseline failures named in LANE-LOG (79), the four known (W19.F3.04 ×2, W22.R1.03,
   realtime.sdkContract's one) and the CRLF-only failures of a Windows worktree are not yours; name what you see.
