# Lane E3: a beat that meets another publish waits for it instead of answering 502

**Status, 2026-10-03: built and tested locally; nothing committed, pushed or deployed.** The fix is in the demo kit's beat route. When a beat's publish meets another publish on the tenant's configuration set, the route waits, reads the base that is current, and publishes again on it. It does this at most three times, over about two seconds, and only for the engine's 409 `publication_conflict`. The engine is unchanged: the learning alarm holds no operation on the set, so there is nothing in `src/config/publication.ts` that could yield (§1).

Results on the local OH rig:
- **Before the fix:** the request-level and DOM suites run one after the other are clean, with the learning alarm publishing lift snapshots between the beats. Presses that meet another publish reproduce the hosted 502 and its exact message: 6 of 36 in the press probe, 6 of 8 in a burst.
- **After the fix:** 36 of 36 and 8 of 8 land. The request-level suite then the DOM suite (beats 11 to 13 included) are clean twice back to back: 224 of 224 and 466 of 466, both times.

The typecheck passes. The full vitest run adds no failure (§5). Built on `7272ebd`.

## 1. Cause

**What the hosted run showed.** On `ba267b3c`, the DOM suite's beat 12 (`12-pin-launch`, a slots publish) answered `POST /oh/api/beat` 502. The message was `Publication conflicts with the retained operation or authored revision set`. The five DOM failures all follow from that one press. The suite's own record of network traffic shows the page sent one beat-12 request, and three beat requests in the whole walk (`/oh/api/beat ×3`). The request-level suite's beats and reset had finished before the DOM suite started.

**The conflict rule.** A demo tenant's reflex, catalog, slots and learn documents are one publication set, and a publish moves that set with a single conditional write of its head. The beat route reads the base (`GET /content/<kind>`) and then writes against it (`PUT` with `If-Match: "<revision>/<set revision>/<set digest>"`). The engine refuses that write with 409 `publication_conflict` when another publish has taken the set in the meantime:
- the head holds another operation's pending set, or a set already committed after the base (`operationAt`, `src/config/publication.ts:432`);
- the committed set is no longer the one the beat read (`:457`);
- the document's revision moved (`:462`);
- the head changed between the engine's read and its conditional write (`:483`).

Each case needs a second publish on the same tenant's set, of either kind. A catalog publish refuses a concurrent slots publish, and the reverse.

**The alarm is not that second publish.** The brief's first hypothesis was that the publication alarm, which E2's fix lets run to completion, holds a retained operation on the set. It does not, and nothing in `publication.ts` could yield:
- **The code.** `LearnStats.publish()` (`src/durable-objects/LearnStats.ts:796`) writes the lift snapshot to R2 at `lift/<tenant>/<brand>/<slot>/<version>.json` and to KV at `lift:<tenant>:<brand>:<slot>`. It only ever reads the publication (`pinPublication` with the serving cache, `committedPublication`). The only writers of `config-publication/v2/<tenant>/head.json` are `publishSet`, `initializePublicationSet` and `recoverPublication`. They are reached from the `/content` and `/config` routes, the item reset, enrichment publishing and the demo bootstrap, never from an alarm, a queue consumer or a cron. The synthetic monitor's environment refuses any write under `config-publication/`.
- **The rig.** With E2's fix in the code, the suites ran with the alarm publishing all through the walk. Before the fix, 56 lift snapshots were archived during the run, and beat 12's commit was followed 2.5 s later by a snapshot and then by beat 13's commit. After the fix, in run 2, a snapshot landed 1.1 s before beat 13's commit. No conflict either time (§3).

**The beat route does not publish on a stale base by itself.** It reads the base immediately before each write, without the serving cache (`readPublication(…, servingCache = false)`). In a sequential walk, every beat lands on its first attempt. That holds both before the fix (the suites passed) and after it (the suites needed no retry).

**The cause: another publish on the `oh` set during beat 12's read and write.** The 502 happens when another publish on the `oh` set lands between beat 12's read and its write, or still holds the set when the beat writes. The route answered the engine's 409 as 502 at once, for a conflict that resolves within one publish. On the rig, presses that meet produce exactly the hosted answer:
- two tabs;
- the same press twice;
- a reset during a press;
- a burst of presses.

**Where the hosted second publish came from cannot be read from the hosted log.** The page sent one beat-12 request. So the other publish came from outside that page. It could have been:
- another client of the public demo worker on tenant `oh`: a person with the store open pressing a beat or a reset, or another lane's run;
- the same POST sent a second time by the transport.

The fix covers all of these, and it does not depend on knowing which one it was.

## 2. The fix, by file

| File | Change |
|---|---|
| `src/demos/kit/actionRoutes.ts` | **`CONFLICT_RETRY_MS = [250, 600, 1150]`** (exported, frozen): three retries, 2.0 s of waiting in all. **`applyBeat`** now loops over a new `publishOnce` (the old read-then-write body, unchanged). It retries only when the write answers **409 with `code: 'publication_conflict'`**. Before each retry it waits, then reads the base again and writes with that read's `If-Match` and a fresh `Idempotency-Key`. Every attempt publishes the worker's own static document: the same closed list of names, the same minted 60-second operator token for the press, the same `/content` pipeline. Nothing is forced, and no other operation is recovered or overwritten. **Every other refusal answers at once**, as before: 422, 403, 428, 503, a 409 with another code or none, and a read failure. **What the answer says:** a first-try publish answers exactly as before. A retried one adds `retried: n`. A publish still refused after three retries answers 502 with the engine's message, `status: 409`, `code: 'publication_conflict'` and `retried: 3`. A write refusal now carries the engine's `code` when it gave one. **`/reset`** retries each of its beats on its own. Each retry logs one warning line with the beat name and the wait, and never a credential |
| `src/demos/kit/actionRoutes.retry.test.ts` (new, 12 tests) | **The policy, with a stand-in pipeline and a fake clock:** the schedule; a conflict retried on the *second* read's base with a fresh operation id and the worker's own document (never the request's); exhaustion after three retries and about two seconds, answering the engine's refusal; seven other refusals answered at once with one read and one write; a first-time publish answered byte for byte as before; a reset retrying one of its beats. **The real `/content` routes on the real publication engine**, over an in-memory R2 whose conditional writes compare per-write etags: a beat alone lands first try; another operator's catalog publish placed between the beat's slots read and write gets the engine's 409, and the beat lands on the new base, with the other catalog preserved; another publish holding the head pending while it finishes is waited for, and the beat lands on retry 2; a head held past the retries answers 502 with the engine's message and leaves the other operation's pending set untouched; the lift snapshot's R2 archive and KV writes between a read and a write never conflict; two presses at once both land |
| `src/config/publication.ts` | Unchanged. The alarm has no operation on the set (§1) |

**The new tests against the route before the fix.** I put HEAD's `actionRoutes.ts` in place, adding only the schedule constant so the import resolves. On that route 7 of the 12 tests fail: the policy tests and the four real-engine conflict tests. The 5 controls pass. With the fix in place, 12 of 12 pass.

**The bound, stated plainly.** In the worst case each round of retries lands one of the presses that met. So up to four presses that meet at once all land. A set held for more than about two seconds still answers 502, with the reason and `retried: 3`. An example is an operation left pending by a writer that died mid-publish: the beat neither waits for it indefinitely nor touches it. On the rig, a burst of eight landed all eight, because the rounds fall out of step. The routes need no login, so the bound matters: a press now makes at most four publish attempts, and only when it meets another publish. Every attempt is one of the demo's named states, as before.
