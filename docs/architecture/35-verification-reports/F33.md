## 3 · Verdict and severity

**Confirmed.** The provisioned topology is per *environment*, not per *customer*, and nothing in the repository provisions, deploys, pins or isolates a second customer.

Stronger than the audit put it: the ambiguity the finding leaves open is closed in the repo's own words, and the audit did not cite it. `wrangler.toml:312` — on the single `[env.production]` block — reads "CORS_ORIGINS and TENANTS are onboarding inputs **per customer**", while `src/tenancy/middleware.ts:26` documents the same `provisioned` list as "The brands this stamp serves". Customer and brand are the same configuration field. And `docs/PS-Implementation-Runbook.md:173` makes the residency promise the topology cannot keep — "**Data residency + erasure:** all shopper state lives inside the customer's stamp" — with §9 adding "Environment = separate stamp, **independently version-pinned** — one customer holds through a code freeze while another upgrades", and §8 defining rollback as "flip the stamp's version pin". No version-pin mechanism exists.

**Severity: blocks the pilot.** I do not agree with "fix before the next customer", and the split matters:

- The *customer-boundary* sentence alone is correctly graded "fix before the next customer": there is one customer today, so no commingling exists yet.
- But the isolation mechanism the topology leans on fails on the pilot's own stamp. `docs/architecture/20-scope-truth-ledger.md:34` row 3 reports scope appendix **§1.7 "multi-brand provisioning with hard data isolation" as ✅ CLOSED 2026-09-03**, with the closing sentence "A request resolves its brand from host or an allow-listed header". The allow-list is the `provisioned` set, not the caller's credential. Probes 2 and 3 show a public site key and an operator token each crossing that boundary the moment a second brand is provisioned — and `docs/architecture/19-tapestry-delivery-ledger.md:126` lists "`kate-spade` tenant provisioned" as CW10 staging content. Per the audit's own scale, "blocks the pilot" covers "a feature claimed in that pilot", and "blocks launch" covers "representations that the full contracted capability is delivered". A contracted capability is reported closed and is not.

**Size: M–L. I agree with the audit.** M (1–2 engineer-weeks) for the deployment contract: templated per-customer wrangler config, a provisioning script taking customer *and* environment, a CI deploy matrix, per-customer secret sets and domains, version pinning, and migrating Coach off the shared workers. L once jurisdiction bindings, permitted stores/exports/logs, connector destinations and failover are included. CW15's 1.5-day budget (`19-tapestry-delivery-ledger.md:101`) is wrong by roughly an order of magnitude.

## 4 · What the audit got wrong

1. **"Five customer regions" is unsourced.** Nothing in the repository asks for five regions. EMEA and Japan are named; five is not. Written as if quoting a requirement.
2. **"Hot learning slots" is imprecise.** `LearnStats` is `{tenant}:{brand}:{slot}` (`src/learn/fan.ts:15`) — per-tenant objects. The real shared hot resources are the single queue consumer, the shared KV/R2/D1, and the un-tenanted rate limiter.
3. **"Cron" invites an overstatement.** Per-tenant jobs are independent `ctx.waitUntil(...).catch(...)`; one tenant's failure does not abort another's. The genuine coupling is one schedule and one invocation budget for everybody.
4. **The `objects.ts:53` citation understates the case.** Citing one line implies jurisdiction is missing *there*. It is missing everywhere: zero occurrences repo-wide.
5. **The strongest citations were left on the table.** `wrangler.toml:312` (customer = a TENANTS entry), `docs/PS-Implementation-Runbook.md:173` and §9 (residency and version-pinning promised to the customer), and the absence of any deploy job in `.github/workflows/ci.yml` against CW15's committed matrix.
6. **"Legal/data-residency obligations were not supplied"** is fair about the brief but incomplete about the repo: the runbook already makes a residency claim to customers.

## 5 · What the audit missed, adjacent to this finding

1. **`X-Tenant` is an unauthenticated brand selector on `/realtime/*`** (probe 2). The very failure `src/tenancy/tenant.ts:106-110` says the `provisioned` list prevents. Bounded to provisioned brands — which is precisely the multi-brand stamp the model prescribes.
2. **`/sort` is not gated at all.** `src/routes/sort.ts:61,94` use `c.get('tenant')`, and `/sort` is behind neither `sdkKey()` nor `operatorWrites()` — so on an enforced stamp an *unauthenticated* caller selects a brand with one header and gets its ranked products.
3. **Operator tokens carry no tenant** (probe 3); `operator_accounts` and `operator_audit` have no tenant column. No per-brand operator authority, and the audit trail cannot attribute a change to a brand.
4. **A typo in `TENANTS` is a silent data-protection failure.** Malformed JSON degrades to coach-only with a `console.warn`; the 03:00 erasure rewrite, the hourly fold and the day report all iterate `tenantConfig(env).provisioned` (`src/index.ts` ≈200-235), so every other brand silently stops being erased — while every request collapses onto Coach's unprefixed key space.
5. **Two different tenant lists drive the crons.** `TREND_ROLLUP_TENANTS` drives the autonomy cycle and the trend rollup; `tenantConfig(env).provisioned` drives fold, day report and erasure. Provision a brand in `TENANTS` and forget the other variable and it silently gets no learning autonomy and no regional priors.
6. **`provision-stamp.sh:50` cannot add a second tenant.** `wrangler secret put SDK_KEYS` *replaces*; re-running with a new `--tenant` destroys the first tenant's key. There is no add-a-tenant path.
7. **The documented default `SDK_KEYS` is `*:demo-site`** (`docs/deployment/01-deploy.md:34`, `scripts/deploy.sh:52`). A `*` owner short-circuits even the `/v1/:tenant/` check (`edgeAccess.ts:101`), so on any worker carrying it, one public key addresses every tenant. Brief §6 files this as a demo-breakage decision, not as an isolation defect.
8. **No per-tenant quota or rate limit** on either customer surface (item 13 above).
9. **The demo tree ships into production** (item 14 above).
10. **A test that passes for the wrong reason.** `src/tenancy/middleware.test.ts:66-77` asserts that `X-Tenant: kate-spade` *is honoured* on a configured stamp — including line 77, where the header overrides the host map on `https://www.coach.com/`. It codifies the vulnerable behaviour as correct. All 75 tests in `src/tenancy/` prove **key-space separation** (where bytes land); none proves **access control** (who may reach a prefix). Ledger 20 row 3 cites them as proof of "hard data isolation", which is an authorization claim they never test.
11. **`docs/deployment/01-deploy.md:57-60` is stale**: it still says `[env.production]` "declares only a name" and that `deploy.sh production` refuses. Production has been fully declared and provisioned since 2026-09-05.
12. **Checked and refuted, in fairness:** shopper ids do *not* collide across brands on a shared salt — `src/identity/shopperId.ts:49` hashes `salt\ntenant\naccountId`. Namespace escape via a crafted `X-Tenant` or visitor id is genuinely blocked (`isValidTenantId`, `assertLogicalKey`). `DecisionRing`/`LearnStats` names are colon-joined with the tenant first, so no escape there either. The `/v1` surface is correctly bound to the path tenant.

## 7 · Remedy assessment

The audit's remedy is **directionally right and correctly scoped for the topology half**: keep shared code, default to the already-recorded per-customer per-environment contract, or explicitly agree a different isolation model and demonstrate it. That is the correct call and it costs nothing to *decide*.

It is **under-scoped in one specific way**: it treats isolation as a deployment-topology property only. Even with stamp-per-customer, the brand boundary *inside* a stamp is unenforced, and that is where the pilot lives. A remedy that ships per-customer stamps and leaves `X-Tenant` as a credential-free brand selector still leaves Coach and Kate Spade able to reach each other's shopper state and operator surfaces.

The residency half is **correctly scoped but not actionable as written**: "define regional shopper home, permitted stores/processing/exports/logs, jurisdiction bindings, failover, and connector destinations" is L-sized work that cannot start before the customer supplies obligations. Right to name; wrong to hold the pilot on.

### Smallest safe fix that closes the launch-blocking part — **S, roughly 1–3 engineer-days**

Bind the resolved tenant to the credential, not to the provisioned list:

1. In `sdkKey()` (`src/middleware/edgeAccess.ts:115-127`), when there is no `:tenant` route param, resolve the tenant with `tenantForRequest(c.env, c.req.raw)` and pass it to `verifySdkKey`. Coach's key plus `X-Tenant: kate-spade` then answers 403 instead of 200. Two lines.
2. Mount `sdkKey()` on `/sort` as well, or resolve `/sort`'s tenant from a credential rather than the header.
3. Add a `tenant` (or `tenants[]`) claim when the operator token is minted, and check it in `operatorWrites()` against `c.get('tenant')`. Interim: refuse `X-Tenant` on `/operator/*` unless the account is explicitly marked cross-brand. Add a `tenant` column to `operator_audit`.
4. Refuse a `*` owner in `SDK_KEYS` when `ENVIRONMENT` is `staging` or `production`.
5. Fix the tests that assert the old behaviour: `src/tenancy/middleware.test.ts:66-77` must become "the header selects a brand **only when the credential owns it**", and add one pair-test per gated surface (`/realtime`, `/sort`, `/operator`) using two non-default tenants, matching the pattern `storeIsolation.test.ts` already establishes.

That closes the cross-brand path without touching the topology decision. The topology decision itself — stamp per customer, or an explicitly agreed shared-SaaS model — should be taken separately and, whichever way it goes, `docs/architecture/20-scope-truth-ledger.md:34` row 3 must be reopened until the access-control half of §1.7 is tested, and `docs/PS-Implementation-Runbook.md:173` and §9 must not be shown to a customer while residency and version pinning are unimplemented.
