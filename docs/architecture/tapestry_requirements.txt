1. Introduction to BTIE
"Every action you take is a vote for the type of person you wish to become."
— James Clear, Atomic Habits
Every product view, every scroll, every second spent on a page tells us something about who this customer is. Not who we think they are based on a segment—but who their behavior reveals them to be.
We (Avinash, Brand teams and Tapestry teams) believe that behavioral targeting is our next biggest Omnichannel growth unlock and on digital channels. Currently, we do minimal behavioral targeting primarily via product recommendations on the website and emails. By adapting the experience based on their behavior we can serve 1-1 experiences (product, content and different layouts/templates) on the website and aim for full customer journey personalization/adapt the experience across all channels. This should drive 40%-60%+ incremental lift in CVR and significant improvements in LTV, which also means 10-15% in new customer acquisition YoY.
In this document, we define what is BTIE (Behavioral Targeting & Intelligence Engine), our current capabilities, future vision, technical capabilities of the intelligence layer required to fully achieve our vision.

1.1 The Proposal: BTIE
"Customize the experience based on initial rules we create from user research and hypothesis. Then let the system learn how millions of people interact with those rules and content elements - within days, it optimizes toward goals we set."
— Avinash Kaushik
BTIE combines two components:
Behavioral Targeting (BT) — Initial rules we create based on user research and our hypothesis. These are our best guesses, encoded as starting points.

Instagram visitors on mobile in evening → show contemporary styles
3+ crossbody views → surface more crossbody options
Sale page entry → emphasize value messaging

Intelligence (I) — The system learns how millions of visitors actually interact with those rules and content elements. Within days, it has enough data to optimize toward the goals we set—conversion, engagement, revenue per visit. It may discover our initial rules were wrong (maybe Instagram evening visitors actually prefer classic styles). It adjusts automatically, without manual rule rewriting.
BT without I is not enough. 
Rules alone cap out—they can't learn that segment A actually behaves like segment B, or that a rule that worked last season doesn't work this season. The Intelligence layer is what makes the system get smarter over time instead of staying static.
1.2 The Power of Scale: Why the “I” Matters 
The transformative power of BTIE comes from scale. Consider a simple scenario: a visitor clicks on handbags, views a black crossbody, then views a second similar style.
What makes this magical is that we have literally tens of millions of visitors to our site. At that massive scale, we have a pattern for what happens when someone clicks on handbags, views the black one, and then a second one. The way we respond to each visitor is the intelligence engine having seen that behavior millions of times and then customizing it for that individual in a way that feels like it was custom built just for them.
And if that visitor reacts to this fourth view differently from all other millions of people on our site, that is one more data point for our intelligence engine to learn from.
The scale at which the thinking is happening, the scale at which the learning is happening to power the BT, is simply humanly impossible. This is a competitive advantage for Tapestry.









The Paradigm Shift: From “Reporting Factory” to “Strategic Engine”
 
(Source: “TMAI #493: Bye Human-Powered Analytics. -P2: BTI, NLP, AIG” — The Marketing < > Analytics Intersect Newsletter, Avinash Kaushik)

This shift from reactive, human-powered analysis to proactive, AI-powered intelligence is what Avinash calls moving from “Reporting Factory” to “Strategic Engine.”
The Old Way (Human-Powered): Massive datasets and dashboards lead to manual analysis, which creates an insight extraction bottleneck. Humans are ill-suited to find needles in haystacks. Insights get missed, remain latent, or are misinterpreted. The outcome: Analyst = Reporter, confusing activity with impact.
The New Way (AI-Powered): AI serves as a force multiplier with core capabilities that transform the equation:
•	Pattern Recognition at Scale — Finds complex, non-linear relationships and hidden clusters
•	Predictive Power — Solves insights latency; tells what’s likely to happen
•	Continuous Learning — Models adapt and refine as new data flows
•	Real-Time Feedback Loop — System learns from outcomes instantly
The outcome: Analyst = Strategist, liberated to focus on impact. This enables activation at scale — dynamic segmentation and personalized interventions that would be impossible with human-powered approaches.
BTIE embodies this paradigm shift. The Intelligence layer delivers exactly these AI core capabilities—pattern recognition across millions of visitor behaviors, predictive power to anticipate what each visitor wants, and continuous learning that gets smarter with every interaction.

3 Future State BTIE: The 3-Click Adaptive Experience 
(adapted and expanded from Avinash’s BTI manifesto)
The Vision: A new visitor feels like the site “gets them” within 3 clicks. Not 10. Not 15. Three. The experience of a great boutique—where the sales associate reads your style before you’ve said a word—delivered at digital scale.
Here’s an example of what that could look like:
Before Click 1 — The Intelligence Already Working:
Sarah taps an Instagram ad for the Tabby bag. Before she’s done anything, we already know: mobile device, Instagram referral, 8pm local time, Northeast US (from IP). But here’s where the “I” kicks in: our intelligence engine has seen millions of visitors with this exact profile—Instagram, mobile, evening, Northeast. It knows that 73%* of them prefer contemporary styles, 62%* browse crossbody bags first, and they convert 2.3x* better when shown lifestyle imagery over product-only shots. The homepage shifts accordingly—contemporary styles rise, the hero features lifestyle content from the collection she came for.

Click 1 — Signal Captured, Pattern Matched:

She taps Handbags—full-price, not Sale. This single click is matched against millions of prior sessions. The intelligence engine knows: visitors who skip Sale and go straight to full-price Handbags have 40%* higher AOV and are 3x* more likely to be considering a gift or special occasion purchase. Crossbody and shoulder bags surface first, not because of a rule we wrote, but because the system learned this is what converts for visitors exhibiting this pattern.
Click 2 — Taste Profile Crystallizes:
She views a black leather crossbody, $450. Now we have her: minimalist aesthetic, structured silhouette, $400-500 range, black preference. The intelligence engine instantly computes her taste embedding—a mathematical representation of her preferences based on this product’s attributes. It then searches across our entire catalog to find items with similar embeddings. The system has learned from millions of sessions that visitors who view this specific product also respond strongly to minimalist styles in shoes (67%* click-through), structured accessories (54%*), and monochromatic ready-to-wear (48%*). The entire site responds—not just “more handbags” but minimalist styles across all categories.
Click 3 — Intent Detection:
A second crossbody, similar style. Profile confirmed. But we also see she’s exploring—bouncing between categories, short dwell times, no cart activity. The intelligence engine recognizes this behavioral signature. Across millions of sessions, it has learned to distinguish “exploring” (See stage) from “considering” (Think stage) from “ready to buy” (Do stage). Sarah’s pattern matches exploring with 89%* confidence. So the experience stays inspirational: lookbooks, style stories, no urgency. Pushing conversion CTAs now would feel pushy and reduce her likelihood of return.
THE POWER OF “I”: Every decision above—which hero image to show, which products to surface, which content to display, whether to show urgency or inspiration—is informed by patterns learned from tens of millions of visitors. Sarah experiences what feels like a personally curated boutique. In reality, she’s benefiting from the collective intelligence of every visitor who came before her. And her unique reactions become data points that make the system smarter for the next visitor.
The Journey Continues — Intent Evolves:
Now either in the same session or in her next visit—she is returning to those same two bags, reading reviews, checking the size guide. The intelligence engine detects the shift: review reading + size guide + return visit = 78%* probability she’s moved from “exploring” to “considering.” The experience shifts accordingly: comparison tools appear, fit/sizing gets highlighted, “Customers say it fits true to size” surfaces (because the system learned this specific concern correlates with size guide views).
By her third visit—adding to cart, checking shipping—she’s deciding. The system recognizes this with 94%* confidence based on cart + shipping check + third visit pattern. Clean path to checkout, a wallet that matches (the intelligence engine knows 34%* of customers who buy this bag also purchase a matching wallet), Apple Pay ready.
This is BTIE. Three clicks to understand her taste. Real-time adaptation as her intent evolves. A site that feels like a stylist who remembers—powered by an intelligence engine that has learned from millions of journeys what works, what converts, and what delights.
What Makes This Different from Today:
•	Today: Sarah is in “Instagram visitor” segment. She sees the same content as all other Instagram visitors.
•	With BTIE: Sarah is an individual. Her experience is dynamically assembled based on her specific signals, matched against patterns from millions of similar journeys, and adapted in real-time as her intent evolves.
•	The difference: Every visitor gets the benefit of collective intelligence. Every interaction makes the system smarter. Scale that is humanly impossible to replicate.
*Assumed Numbers to explain concept

The #1 barrier to success of this project is Content. Intelligence is second.
BTIE can only personalize what exists. Without a rich content library, even the smartest algorithm has nothing to work with. This gives Brand teams critical skin in the game.

5. BTIE design principles and Capabilities needed
5.1 Why 3 Clicks? (And Why It's Possible)
Traditional personalization has two problems: it takes too long to learn (cold-start), and it doesn't adapt fast enough when behavior changes (real-time intelligence). Most systems need 10-15 interactions before they're useful—and even then, they update in batch overnight, missing the signals happening right now.
Leading companies have solved both:
•	Airbnb uses listing embeddings—click on a treehouse, they instantly know you might like other unique stays. Real-time, from the first interaction.
•	Pinterest uses visual similarity—click on a minimalist kitchen, and the entire feed shifts to minimalist content within seconds.
•	Netflix combines what they know about similar users with content attributes—personalized before you've watched anything and continuously adapting as you browse.
The common thread: learn fast, adapt faster. Use what you know about similar visitors as a starting point, enrich with every signal, and update in real-time—not overnight. 
The system addresses three fundamental challenges:
(1) capturing and processing user signals in real-time with sub-10ms latency, 
(2) generating meaningful personalization from sparse initial interactions, and 
(3) scaling to handle millions of concurrent users while maintaining consistent performance.

5.2 Key Design Principles
1.	One visitor, one profile: Anonymous and registered users each het a unique profile built on their behavior/interactions. When possible, recognize the same person (across devices, browsers, and sessions. Whether they browse on mobile, buy on desktop, or return weeks later, all their behavior feeds into a single unified profile.
2.	Learn immediately, not overnight: When a user clicks something, update their profile in milliseconds. Don't wait for nightly batch jobs.
3.	Recency-weighted signals: Recent behavior dominates; preferences decay over time. A click from 30 seconds ago carries more weight than one from 30 days ago. Exponential decay functions ensure the system responds to evolving intent within a session.
4.	Multi-modal signal fusion: Combine implicit signals (scroll depth, hover duration, dwell time) with explicit signals (clicks, add-to-cart, purchases). Each signal type has calibrated weights reflecting its predictive power. Implicit signals often reveal intent before explicit actions.
5.	Similar users like similar things: When you know nothing about a new user, find users who behave like them and use their preferences as a starting point.
6.	Explore-exploit balance: Continuously test new content while leveraging known preferences. Without exploration, the system only reinforces existing assumptions and never discovers new user interests. Multi-armed bandits ensure every session includes controlled experimentation.
7.	Graceful degradation: When personalization systems fail or have insufficient data, fall back to popularity-based, trending, or editorially curated recommendations. Users should never see empty states or errors; the experience degrades gracefully to a reasonable default.
8.	Policy & Governance Layer: We need a policy layer in the system to explicitly govern what the models may do. This is an absolutely must for success . Guardrails (e.g. No click-bait patterns , No over-emphasis on discount signals, diversity, legal, merchandizing commitments, inventory available  etc. see A.4 for details), Data minimization, consent management, and right-to-be-forgotten capabilities built into the architecture from day one. Compliance with GDPR, CCPA, and emerging regulations is a foundational requirement, not an afterthought. 
9.	Embedding-first architecture: Semantic vector representations of users and products enable similarity matching even with sparse data. New users can be matched to similar users; new products inherit relevance from similar items. Critical for cold-start resolution.

5.3 Capabilities Needed and Phased Approach to BTIE
This section outlines our recommended approach to building BTIE—informed by vendor evaluations, industry research, and technical feasibility assessment.
Three Types of Recommendations
Personalization delivers value through three distinct recommendation types. Each requires different ML approaches and is at a different level of maturity:
Type	What Gets Personalized	Current State
Product	Which products surface, in what order. Includes "You May Also Like," cross-sells, and cart recommendations.	Existing vendor provides 1:1 ML-based recommendations. We have a serviceable solution in place (which can be further enhanced/customized in Phase 2)

Product recommendations are table stakes — every ecommerce site has them. The differentiation opportunity is in content and experience. That’s where the 3-click BTIE vision lives.
5.3.1 Phase 1: Capabilities needed to build Content & Experience Recommendation System
While we can continue using the existing recommendation system for product recommendations for initial phases, we need to build a new AI/ML system specifically for content and experience personalization.
Why build?
We evaluated 10+ vendors—established industry leaders and emerging startups. All of them support product recommendations well but none of them offer real-time, ML-driven content or experience personalization at the individual level. The best they offer is segment-based rules, which is what we already have. This capability doesn’t exist off the shelf; we need to build it.
Capabilities needed to build Content & Experience Recommendation System

6. Measurement & Evaluation 
6.1 Why BTIE Measurement is Different
Traditional A/B testing assumes a simple setup: randomly assign visitors to treatment or control, measure outcomes, compare. This works when the treatment is static. BTIE breaks this assumption. The system is non-stationary - it learns and adapts continuously. A bandit algorithm that showed Product A to User 1 on Monday might show Product B to a similar user on Wednesday, because it learned something in between. The treatment itself is constantly changing.
Consider what happens if we try traditional measurement. In Week 1, the bandit is exploring, showing many options, and conversion hovers around 2.1%. By Week 4, the bandit has learned and shows winners more often - conversion reaches 3.8%. If we take a snapshot at Week 2 and compare to control, we understate impact. If we take a snapshot at Week 4, we may overstate it. The system state at any point in time is a function of everything that came before. This is why BTIE is fundamentally a causal inference problem, not a simple experimentation problem.
6.2 The Analytics Flywheel: Predict, Optimize, Learn
Our measurement philosophy follows the Analytics Flywheel model: Predict (win before you spend), Optimize (win while you spend), and Learn (did we win?). Most analytics teams spend their entire lives in the Learn phase - looking at data after the fact. We aim higher. BTIE measurement spans all three phases, with each feeding the next in a continuous cycle.
Phase	BTIE Application	Key Questions
PREDICT	Pre-set targets for each phase. Define success criteria before spending. Content pre-testing.	What lift do we need to justify investment? What does success look like?
OPTIMIZE	In-flight monitoring via decision trees. Automated triggers for intervention. Content rotation based on live results.	Is the system converging? Are we within expected bounds? Should we intervene?
LEARN	Controlled experiments with holdouts. Incrementality measurement. Post-campaign analysis with three biggest learnings.	What was the true incremental impact? What would have happened without BTIE?

The power of this flywheel is that each rotation makes the next better. Learnings from post-campaign analysis feed into better predictions for the next phase. Invent once, scale infinitely - the ML models that power BTIE get smarter with each cycle, compounding returns over time.
6.3 Methodology Over Metrics: Attribution is Not Incrementality
Before discussing specific metrics, we must address how we measure. The methodology matters more than the metric. We could track every touchpoint, build sophisticated multi-touch attribution models, and still have no idea whether BTIE actually drove incremental business value.
Attribution tells us who touched what. Incrementality tells us what would have happened without the intervention. This distinction is everything.
We could observe that BTIE users convert at 4.2% while showing them personalized content, and attribute that revenue to BTIE. But what if those same users would have converted at 3.8% anyway? The incrementality - the actual value BTIE created - is only the delta: 0.4 percentage points. Without proper methodology, we risk claiming credit for conversions that would have happened regardless.
Our methodological commitment: We will measure BTIE using true test-control incrementality with controlled experiments. This is the only methodology that Finance will accept, because it is the only one that establishes true causality. We will use 90%+ statistical significance thresholds. We will always have pre-set targets defined before any money is spent.
6.4 Measurement Framework
6.4.1 Holdout Groups (The Foundation)
The most reliable measurement approach: maintain a consistent control population that never receives BTIE optimization. This creates a true counterfactual - what would have happened without BTIE - against which we measure incremental impact.
Implementation specifications:
•	Size: 5-10% of traffic held out permanently (not rotating)
•	Assignment: Based on persistent identifier (customer ID for logged-in, device fingerprint for anonymous)
•	Duration: Holdout remains constant for the entire measurement period (6+ months minimum)
•	Experience: Holdout sees current production experience (segment-based rules)
Why permanent holdout matters: Rotating holdouts create the same non-stationarity problem we are trying to avoid. A user who was in BTIE for 3 months and then moves to holdout carries learned behaviors with them - they are no longer a valid counterfactual.
The trade-off: At 5% holdout with projected 40-60% CVR lift, we are sacrificing ~2-3% of potential incremental conversions for measurement confidence. This is the cost of knowing whether BTIE actually works.
6.4.2 Pre-Set Targets (Always Pre-Set)
Before any phase launches, we define what success looks like. This is non-negotiable. Pre-set targets prevent post-hoc rationalization and ensure honest evaluation of results.
Metric	Minimum Success	Target	Stretch
CVR Lift vs. Holdout	+10%	+40%	+60%
Revenue Per Visitor Lift	+10%	+25%	+40%
Return Rate Delta	<+5%	No change	-5%

6.4.3 Aggregate Outcomes Over Time Windows
Instead of point-in-time comparisons, we measure cumulative business impact over defined windows. This approach accounts for the learning curve inherent in adaptive systems and smooths out the noise that would make snapshot measurements misleading.
Reporting cadence:
•	Monthly (YoY and MoM): CVR, RPV, Engagement - operational monitoring, catch issues early
•	Quarterly: Revenue lift, Margin, Return rate - business performance, smooth out noise
•	Bi-Anually: LTV indicators, Repeat purchase - relationship metrics, long-term validation
6.5 Reporting with IABI: Insights, Actions, Business Impact
Every report we deliver follows the IABI framework. The purpose of a dashboard is 80% to recommend actions and 20% to provide data. The more senior the leader, the higher the chance they need more than data to understand performance. We never send data without context.
For every metric we report:
1.	Insight - Not what the data shows (they can see that), but why it shows that. The explanation for performance that is not visible in the chart.
2.	Action - Specific recommendation. Never we need more analysis - that is our job. Actions are specific: Increase content pool for Category X by 20 variants or Extend Phase 1 by 2 weeks to reach statistical significance.
3.	Business Impact - Predicted impact if action is taken. This creates urgency. Implementing this change will deliver an estimated $180K/month in incremental revenue. Leaders can re-sort actions by impact and prioritize accordingly.

This measurement framework ensures we know whether BTIE is working - not just whether it is running. By committing to the Analytics Flywheel (Predict, Optimize, Learn), incrementality methodology over attribution, and IABI-driven reporting, we build the CFO-proof measurement capability that transforms BTIE from an experiment into a proven growth engine. Data not as a side-show. Data as an influential core.

The Content Ranker scores every piece of content against every user. Five factors determine the final score:
Factor	What It Measures	Weight	Why
Style Similarity	How well content matches user's aesthetic preferences	35%	Primary driver
Journey Fit	Does content match where user is in buying journey?	25%	Wrong stage = bounce
Engagement Prediction	How likely is this user to click/engage based on similar users?	25%	Learned from data
Freshness	How new is content + has user seen it before?	10%	Avoid fatigue
Exploration	Bonus for content we're still learning about	5%	Enables learning
A.3.4 Journey Stage × Content Matrix
Different journey stages require different content strategies:
Journey Stage	Content That Works	Content to Avoid	Goal
Exploring	Lookbooks, trend stories, inspiration galleries, brand stories, lifestyle content, broad category features	Aggressive CTAs, urgency messaging, discount offers, comparison content	Inspire & engage
Considering	Product comparisons, detailed features, styling how-tos, care guides, customer reviews, size/fit content	Generic inspiration content, brand manifestos, unrelated categories	Inform & validate
Deciding	Product-specific styling, in-stock alerts, delivery info, complementary products, easy checkout paths	Long-form editorial, exploration content, new category introductions	Convert & reassure
Post-Purchase	Care instructions, styling tips for purchase, complementary products, loyalty benefits, brand community	Same-product promotions, aggressive upsells, discount offers	Delight & retain
A.3.5 Multi-Armed Bandit for Content
Instead of A/B testing content and waiting weeks for results, BTIE uses multi-armed bandits for continuous optimization:
Aspect	How It Works
Algorithm	Thompson Sampling—balances showing content we know works (exploitation) vs testing new content (exploration)
Learning Speed	New content gets ~10% of impressions initially. If it performs well, share increases. Poor performers fade within 48-72 hours.
Personalized Arms	Bandits run per-audience segment (not globally). Content that works for minimalist explorers may differ from maximalist deciders.
Reward Signal	Primary: Click-through rate. Secondary: Dwell time >30s, scroll depth >50%, downstream conversion within session.
Cold Content Handling	New content starts with prior from similar content (same style cluster). Gets exploration bonus until we have 500+ impressions.
A.3.6 Content Metadata Requirements
For ML-driven content selection, editorial content requires enhanced metadata:
Metadata Field	Example Values	Use in Model
style_cluster	classic, contemporary, minimalist, maximalist	Primary matching against user style preferences
journey_stage_fit	[explore, consider] or [decide]	Filter content inappropriate for user's journey stage

content_embedding	64-dimensional vector	Pre-computed for real-time similarity scoring
freshness_date	2026-01-10	Recency scoring, seasonal relevance
min_impressions	1000 (for hero), 500 (for modules)	Ensure new content gets sufficient exploration

A.4  Policy & Governance Layer 
We need a policy layer in the system to explicitly govern what the models may do. This is an absolutely must for success.
Policy Constraints Include:
•	No click-bait patterns — System will not optimize toward sensationalized or misleading content
•	No over-emphasis on discount signals — Protect brand equity by preventing discount-led optimization
•	Diversity constraints — Ensure representation across product categories, brands, price points
•	Legal compliance — Adhere to advertising standards, privacy regulations (GDPR, CCPA)
•	Merchandizing commitments — Honor pre-committed brand placements and campaign commitments
•	Inventory constraints — Don’t over-promote products with limited inventory availability
