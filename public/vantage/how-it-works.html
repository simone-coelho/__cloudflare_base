For engineers · the arithmetic behind the Vantage demo

The arithmetic, with the constants on sliders

What this page is

This page shows the arithmetic behind what the Vantage demo puts on screen: how a shopper’s interest is kept, how every candidate in a slot is scored against it, how a stranger starts from her region’s lean, and how learning is counted behind a dial that starts at zero. Every number it computes is recomputed in your browser from the documents the demo publishes and its labelled synthetic regional sales, each formula cited to the code it mirrors; the demo’s own decisions come from the service, and the figures the service returned are quoted beside them, marked as measured. Read it from the top down: each section explains its idea in words and formulas first, then shows its instruments, with a short paragraph above them saying what you are looking at, what to try and what to watch for. Every group of controls and every instrument carries a circled question mark; hover over it, focus it or tap it to see what it is, the constant it sets, where that constant is published, and what moves when it changes.

1The one idea

The unit of state is the dimension value, not the click

The engine does not remember products: per shopper it keeps a small vector of graded interests, one coordinate per dimension value she has touched (brand samsung, ecosystem android, price band core), each a number between 0 and 1. Products, promotions and page modules carry attributes in the same dimensions, so a candidate’s score is its tags dotted with her vector, weighted by the slot it competes in. Nothing is configured per product: an offer published a minute ago is scored the moment it carries tags, and one view of one phone moves every candidate that shares any of its values.

What you are looking at: one candidate’s score, taken apart. Each row is one tag of the Galaxy S26 offer, with her interest in that tag’s value (read from her vector), the deals slot’s weight for the tag’s dimension, and their product, a × w; the bottom line adds the rows into the candidate’s score. Try: find a row that scores and one that does not, and see which of its two numbers is zero. Watch for: a tag scores only when she has some interest in its value and the slot weighs its dimension; the values in her vector that this offer does not carry are listed under it, because they score other candidates.

Why this unit

Systems that keep state per event or per item usually need something learned per item before a view of one item says anything about another: a co-occurrence table, a similarity model, a rule per product. Keyed on the dimension value, one Galaxy view raises every Android candidate, including one nobody has been shown yet, and the receipt can name the coordinate that did it.

Read quickly, the mechanism sounds like “she clicked Samsung, so show Samsung”. The sections below show where the arithmetic goes further than that sentence: interest is graded and decays (2); one click moves several coordinates (3); the slot weighs them (4); a stranger starts from a prior that gives way (5); and each dimension forgets at its own speed (6).

2Interest is a quantity, not a flag

How much evidence, and how fresh

A flag records that something happened. A quantity records how much evidence there is and how old it is, which is what lets interest rise with repetition, fade with time, and leave an audience at a moment that can be computed in advance. Each coordinate stores two numbers, the raw evidence R and the time of the last touch t. Nothing is decayed in storage: every read computes the decay from the clock, so a replay of the same events gives the same answer (src/reflex/core.ts:11-19).

Reff = R · e−Δt/τ

The evidence left now: what was stored, faded by a factor of e for every τ since the last touch.

src/reflex/core.ts:290-293

R ← Reff + w

A touch adds its action’s weight to whatever is left. The registry Vantage publishes weighs a view or a page view 1, a search 1.5, a selector, filter or compare 2, a cart add or a started order 3, a purchase 5. The engine’s shipped table names only views, wishlists, cart adds, purchases and the content events, so an untuned tenant weighs the other six 0; an action no table names weighs 0. A custom event is named by its data.action, data.eventName or data.event, so a selector sent as custom weighs 2.

src/reflex/core.ts:574, :589-590 · the shipped table: core.ts:221-244 · the published table: scripts/seed-vantage.mjs:113-125 at c720907 · the name: src/reflex/contentTelemetry.ts:77-83

a = Reff / (Reff + K)

Interest is the evidence over the evidence plus a constant. It is ½ when R = K and never reaches 1, so more evidence always counts, by less each time.

src/reflex/core.ts:296-298 · K = 1.8: core.ts:260, scripts/seed-vantage.mjs:127 at c720907

enter when a ≥ θin; leave when a < θout

Two lines, not one, so a shopper sitting near a line does not flap in and out of an audience on every read: she joins at 0.60 (evidence R ≥ 2.7) and stays until she falls under 0.45 (R < 1.4727). Time alone can make her leave, never join. The validator refuses a document whose θout is not below its θin.

src/reflex/core.ts:618-628 · 0.60 / 0.45: core.ts:261-262, scripts/seed-vantage.mjs:128-129 at c720907 · the validator: src/reflex/configStore.ts:157-161

t* = t + τ · ln( R (1 − θout) / (K θout) )

The instant she leaves if nothing else happens, solved in closed form, so an alarm can be set for it instead of polling.

src/reflex/core.ts:703-731 (the floor at :717, t* at :726)

What you are looking at: one coordinate of one shopper, for example brand samsung, on the published constants. The sliders set K, the memory τ, the two audience lines and the weight of the next touch. The first chart shows her interest against the number of touches, with no time between them; the second shows it against time as you press; under them are the readout and the three worked numbers. Try: press the red button three times quickly, then stop. Watch for: the third press carrying her over the entry line, the dashed projection fading once you stop, and t*, the instant she leaves the audience, worked out in advance rather than found by polling.

3One click, four coordinates

A product view is a touch on every dimension the item carries

The Galaxy S26 as the catalog lists it: brand samsung, ecosystem android, $899.99, category phones. The engine derives the price band from price_usd with the registry’s own cuts, $400 and $900, so $899.99 is core and the same phone at 512 GB ($1,099.99) is premium. One view is therefore four touches, and each lands on a coordinate with its own memory.

What you are looking at: one product view, three ways. First, the event the page sends when she views a phone, then the touches the engine takes from it; under them, a live shopper: a row of phone buttons, a meter for every coordinate she has, and her whole stored state as a table. Try: press the Galaxy S26, then the Pixel 11. Watch for: four meters moving on each press; ecosystem android, price band core and category phones taking a second touch from the Pixel while brand splits across two makers; and the table holding only two numbers per value, the evidence and the time of its last touch. This shopper carries on into sections 4 and 5.

4Scoring 10,000 candidates

    
Nothing per product; everything per dimension and per slot

    
Every candidate in a slot is scored the same way: for each of its tags, her interest in that value times the slot’s weight for that dimension, summed. The weights belong to the slot, so the same interest in samsung counts 0.2 in the homepage deals module and 1 in the phones rail. Then the slot’s rules take the top N: pinned pieces first and never ranked, then the ranking, with a diversity cap under which a third piece of one brand yields its place to the next ranked piece. A pin is a required prefix: if the pinned piece is not eligible for the request, the whole slot serves nothing, which is the engine’s current behaviour. Only slots are decided: the buy box is not a slot, and the engine is never asked about it.

    

      

        
score = Σtags (d, v) a[d][v] × wslot[d]

        
For each tag on the candidate: her interest in that value, times what this slot says that dimension is worth (0 to 1). Two values on one dimension both count; there is no per-tag weight, mean or normalisation. A dimension the slot does not weigh contributes nothing; a value she has no interest in contributes nothing.

What you are looking at: one slot scored in full, for the shopper from section 3. Choose the homepage deals module or the phones rail. For deals, the controls set how it scores (as served, or the tag sum alone), the intent the page asserts, the diversity cap, a pin and the slot’s weights; the four containers show what it would serve, and the table writes out every candidate’s sum, term by term. 5Cold start as a prior

    
A stranger is scored on her region’s lean until her own evidence outweighs it

    
A stranger’s vector is empty, so every tag sum is zero and the slot would answer its catalogue order. Instead of a second model for strangers, the engine blends a population prior into a copy of her vector before scoring, at a weight that falls as her own evidence grows. The population here is the tenant’s own regional sales. In this demo they are Vantage’s, synthetic, labelled synthetic → warehouse wherever they appear; in production they are the customer’s own sales from its warehouse.

    

      

        
λ = k / (k + Σa)

        
How much the population speaks: 1 for a stranger, falling as the sum of all her own interests, on every dimension, grows. k is kBlend, 0.5 in this demo: Σa = 0.5 gives λ = 0.5 and Σa = 3 gives 0.14. With no learn document at all, the compiled default blends at 1; a published learn document without a regional block turns the prior off.

        
src/reflex/regionTrend.ts:217-222 · read at src/content/service.ts:454 · off without the block: service.ts:444 · the compiled default: service.ts:375, src/content/kinds.ts:684-693 · kBlend: scripts/seed-vantage.mjs:633 at c720907

      

      

        
ã = (1 − λ) · a + λ · share

        
Her blended interest in a value: her own, plus the region’s share of that value, mixed by λ. Her stored vector is never written to; the blend is a copy made for this decision.

        
src/reflex/regionTrend.ts:238-252 · src/content/service.ts:458-460

      

      

        
share = Rvalue / Rleader

        
Within each dimension the region’s leading value is 1 and the others are fractions of it: shares, not probabilities. Only the dimensions the tenant names blend (here brand, ecosystem, price band, category); a dimension left out takes nothing from the region and keeps (1 − λ) of her own. The region’s counts come from the edge’s region on every scored event, on a 24-hour memory; an asserted region never feeds them.

region → country → everyone, at ≥ 30 events

6Two memories, two speeds

    
A claim that fades in a minute beside a lean that holds for weeks

    
τ is set per dimension, so one shopper carries both. In this room brand and content type forget on τ = 60 s; price band, intent and perk on 10 minutes; ecosystem, lines and category keep the 14-day default. Stop pressing and the brand claim withdraws while the ecosystem lean holds. That is why the exit beat withdraws the Samsung claim without reordering the four: the Samsung offers lose their brand term and keep their ecosystem term, and ecosystem still carries them. Measured on the demo worker, the four held their order at the exit; the demo never promises a reorder there.

    
Compressed for the room; production runs days. The engine’s defaults are 14 days, and 35 on price band. The design proposes 14 days on intent, 60 on price band and 90 on ecosystem and lines, phones being financed over 36 months; the validator caps τ at 90 days. Nothing has been measured at production τ.

    
Per-dimension τ: src/reflex/core.ts:39-43, :276-283 · the room’s values: scripts/seed-vantage.mjs:92-112 at c720907 · the defaults: core.ts:259 (14 days), :214 (price band, 35 days) · the cap: src/reflex/configStore.ts:134 · the proposal: the demo’s design document, §4

    
What you are looking at: one shopper with two memories on one clock: brand forgets on 60 s in this room, and ecosystem keeps the 14-day default. The two buttons view a Galaxy or a Pixel, each touching its brand and ecosystem android together; the chart shows the three interests over time with the two audience lines, and the meters show each claim and when it leaves. Try: press the Galaxy three times close together, then stop and let a minute pass (the clock speed helps). Watch for: brand samsung falling under 0.45 and leaving while ecosystem android holds. That is the exit beat, and why the four usually keep their order there.

7Learning what works

    
A counted multiplier behind a dial that starts at zero

    
The second term is a count, not a model. For each item in each cell of context the engine keeps decayed exposures n and successes s on the same lazy decay as the vector, with a 21-day horizon. It pulls the item’s rate toward the slot’s rate in that cell, turns the ratio into a clamped multiplier, and raises it to a trust dial γ, from 0 to 1 per slot. In this demo γ is 0 on every slot: once a cell has a learned lift it is computed and recorded on the receipt and changes nothing until someone turns the dial. On stage no cell has a lift in force yet, so the receipts read lift: null. Exploration is off.

    

      

        
p̂ = (s + n0 · p0) / (n + n0)

        
The item’s rate in this cell, pulled toward the slot’s rate p0 with the strength of n0 = 30 observations, so a handful of lucky clicks cannot move it far.

        
src/learn/stats.ts:449 · n0: stats.ts:20, scripts/seed-vantage.mjs:636 at c720907

      

      

        
p0 = (sslot + n0 · p0,parent) / (nslot + n0)

        
The slot’s own rate in the cell, pulled toward the next coarser cell’s rate the same way.

        
src/learn/stats.ts:411-420

      

      

        
lift = clamp(p̂ / p0, 0.5, 2)

        
How this item does against the slot in the same cell, never more than double and never less than half.

        
src/learn/stats.ts:450

      

      

        
score = base × liftγ

        
At γ = 0 any lift to the power 0 is 1, so the learned term is computed, recorded and ignored. At γ = 1 it applies in full.

        
src/content/decide.ts:365 · the dial: decide.ts:61-62, src/content/kinds.ts:628 · default 0: src/content/service.ts:490 · no lift in force: decide.ts:353, :361 · γ = 0: scripts/seed-vantage.mjs:621 at c720907

      

      

        
everyone → channel → + visit → + stage → + region → + her leading interest

        
Six cells from coarse to fine: the channel (direct, paid_social, paid_search, email, organic, referral), the visit bucket (1, 2–3, 4+), the stage (early, mid, late), the edge’s region, and her leading interest above θin. The finest cell where the item has at least 30 exposures answers, so thin contexts borrow from wider ones instead of waiting.

        
src/learn/stats.ts:37-50, :483-496 (the test at :491) · the cell: src/content/cell.ts:51-72

      

    

    
What you are looking at: one item in one cell of context, with every input yours to set: its exposures and clicks, the slot’s rate, the shrinkage strength, the trust dial γ and the score it multiplies. The readout gives the pooled rate, the lift, and the score before and after the dial; the chart shows the pooled rate moving toward the item’s own rate as exposures grow; the ladder shows which of six cells has enough exposures to answer. Try: give the item many clicks on few exposures, then on many; then turn γ up from 0. 8The knobs are the model

    
Three published documents decide everything above

    
No model is called at decision time; every term on the receipt is arithmetic over the published documents. What decides is three documents: the dimension registry, the slots, and what the slots learn. Every write is validated whole, stamped with a revision, attributed and kept. A rollback writes an old body forward as a new revision, so the history is never rewritten. “Effective immediately” means without a deployment: the writing isolate at once, every other isolate within its 30-second read cache.

    
The routes: src/routes/content.ts:3-14, src/routes/config.ts:206-212 · write and rollback: src/config/versionedStore.ts:108-118 · the stamped version and the cache: src/reflex/configStore.ts:30-38, src/config/publication.ts:8, :296 · rollback as a new revision: configStore.ts:591-598 · the documents below: as served on the demo worker, reflex r3, slots r51, learn r3 (the demo’s facts sheet, §10), identical to scripts/seed-vantage.mjs at c720907

    
What you are looking at: the three documents as served on the demo worker, each line of JSON beside a note in words: the dimension registry, the slots, and what the slots learn. The first is open; the other two open with a click. Try: find K and the two audience lines in the registry, the deals slot’s weights in the slots, and kBlend and gamma in what the slots learn. Watch for: every constant the instruments above take from these documents appearing here as one line, which its hint names by document and field; the rail’s dials, sent on each request, and the catalog’s tags and rules, shown in section 4, are the exceptions. Nothing here is configured per product.

    

  


  
  

    
9The receipt

10What we need from the customer, in this use case

    
Attributes, a mapping, and events that carry the product id

    
Per product: attributes. Per slot: weights and rules. Nothing else is configured.

    
Per slot: the weights, the take, the stage rule, the seeds, the merchandising dial, the diversity cap, pins, exclusions and the learning dials. Per piece of content: its tags, window, eligibility rule, merchandising signals and stage fit.

    
What you are looking at: the three things we need from the customer in this use case, each with what we use it for. The second carries two tables: one line per dimension, naming the feed field it reads and how, and a promotion feed’s fields with what the engine does with each. Try: hold each line against your team’s product feed: which field would fill it, spelled the registry’s way. Watch for: the last column of the second table; several fields decide eligibility or are only displayed, and are never scored.

    
    
      
  1. What we need: attributes in the registry’s vocabulary. Each phone, offer and module carries values on the registered dimensions, exactly as the registry spells them: brand: samsung, not Samsung Electronics. On a dimension the tenant’s catalog names values on, a value outside them is simply not scored. The event’s answer names the product ids the engine does not know; it does not flag an unknown attribute value. A content piece needs id, customerContentId, type, title, tags (dimension to a list of values) and slotTypes; a window, an eligibility rule (1 to 64 regions, 1 to 8 context signals), merchandising signals from 0 to 1, a stage fit, featured products and a stock flag are optional. The offer book (terms, badges, calls to action) goes to the page, never to the engine.
    
        
    What we use them for: every attribute is a tag. A candidate’s score is her interest in each of its tag values times the slot’s weight for that dimension (section 4), so a piece is scored the moment it carries tags, with nothing configured per product.
